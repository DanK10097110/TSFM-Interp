\begin{abstract}%
Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) are built on very different architectures, from decoder-only stacks over 32-step
patches to encoder--decoders over quantized single time steps, yet little is known about whether they learn similar
internal representations. We compare seven TSFMs on a common time axis measured from each model's token spans. The causal
instruments are validated on a forecaster with planted concepts, and registered claims are tested once on sealed
held-out splits of two independently generated corpora. All 21 model pairs share representational geometry above a
shuffled-series null on held-out data, and features in unrelated architectures respond to the same input series. All 20
registered concept-transfer claims confirm on both corpora and on 800 raw real-world windows. The causal structure,
by contrast, is largely model-specific. Causal features act mainly on the level and seasonality of the forecast, and
features that raise level, lower level or damp seasonality occur in all seven models, but no fine-grained concept spans
more than three of them, and the same effect on the same series is detected only between two Chronos models. Single
features are also weakly specific, exceeding random directions drawn from the activation distribution only 1.29-fold.
Several practices common in language-model interpretability give misleading results on these models. Averaging the
tokens within a time window understates TimesFM's held-out CKA with fine-token models by 0.30--0.43, 83.5\% of the most
prominent dictionary features have no held-out causal effect, attention to seasonal lags does not predict which heads
carry seasonality, and a plausible ablation null inflates the number of causal features 2.7-fold. Code, benchmarks and
reproduction commands for every result are public.
\end{abstract}
