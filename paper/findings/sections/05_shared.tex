\section{Finding 1: The Models Agree on What Matters in a Series}
\label{sec:shared}

\subsection{Representational geometry is shared}
\label{sec:geometry}

With concatenated windows, peak CKA (the maximum over layer pairs) between TimesFM and each other model is 0.358--0.635
on v2 and 0.375--0.712 on v3 (512 series each), and all 21 model pairs exceed their shuffled-series null on both corpora
($p=0.001$, 1{,}000 permutations; null 95th percentiles 0.003--0.51, highest for pairs with Time-MoE). The layer pairs
selected on v3 dev were registered and tested once on a fresh sealed split: all 21 exceed their nulls (Holm $p=0.021$),
with TimesFM/Chronos-2 at 0.707 [0.679, 0.734] against 0.712 on dev (\fid{SH-01}). Linear maps between models agree
(below). Architectures with different tokenizers, depths and training objectives thus organize series in measurably
similar ways, a time-series counterpart of the convergence reported for vision and language \citep{huh2024platonic}.

The similarity is invisible to the usual comparison. Averaging a window's tokens, the standard way to compare models
with different token widths, lowers TimesFM's held-out CKA with Chronos-2, Chronos-T5-Base, Chronos-Bolt and Sundial by
0.322, 0.433, 0.301 and 0.303 (each 95\% interval above 0.26; Holm $p=0.0008$; \fid{MN-38}), confirmed for all four
pairs on the second sealed v3 split. The reason is that fine-token models encode within-window order that a coarse-token
model keeps inside one token: in the first block of Chronos-T5-Base, a window's slope decodes with $R^2=0.13$ from the
window mean and 0.96 from finer sub-windows. Pairs with Timer change little, because its 96-step token has no
within-window order to match. Concatenation also raises the stitching gain, from 0.498 to 0.554 on average and from
0.307 to 0.788 for Chronos-T5-Base$\to$TimesFM, while keeping the ranking of pairs (Spearman 0.92).

\subsection{Different architectures select the same inputs}
\label{sec:inputs}

Geometry compares whole layers. Concept transfer asks a sharper question: for a concept defined by the series on which
one model's causal feature fires, does another model contain a feature that singles out the same series? On v2 dev,
1{,}296 of 1{,}584 atlas transfer tests are reciprocal after Benjamini--Hochberg correction. Twenty transfer claims were
registered from two source concepts, TimesFM layer 18 (14 claims) and Sundial layer 6 (6 claims), with destinations in
all seven models. All 20 confirm on the 971 private series at the Holm floor attainable for 20 claims at 2{,}000 null
draws (0.0100), with AUCs of 0.919--0.970 against null 95th percentiles of 0.70--0.75 (Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}b). On
v3, a new set of 20 claims from three source models (Sundial 14, Chronos-2 5, Time-MoE 1) confirms 20 of 20 on the private
split and 20 of 20 on the 800 raw GIFT-Eval windows, with AUC 0.757--0.991 (\fid{SH-18}). The result does not depend on
the alignment grid: re-pooled at 64 and 96 steps, the v3 reciprocal rate moves only from 0.827 to 0.801 and 0.799
(\fid{SH-23}). Only 8 of the 20 best-matching private features on v2 are the ones selected on dev, so the confirmed claim
is that the destination layer contains \emph{some} feature selecting the same series, not that a particular feature
does.

\subsection{The same broad effects recur in every model}
\label{sec:families}

The causal features of all seven models fall into the same few kinds of effect on the forecast
(Figure~\ref{fig:families}). Nine families tile 192 of the 194 v2 causal features. The two largest act on level:
\emph{level raisers} (45 features) and \emph{level lowerers} (44), together 89 of the 192. \emph{Seasonality dampeners}
(25) and \emph{amplifiers} (19) follow; three mixed-effect families (14, 13 and 11), \emph{trend boosters} (11) and
\emph{volatility dampeners} (10) are smaller. Level raisers, level lowerers and seasonality dampeners each have causal
members in all seven models, and volatility dampeners in six. Membership reflects each feature's own measured effect, so
the recurrence does not depend on how the families are cut. Together with shared inputs, this says that the seven
models attend to the same properties of a series---its level and its seasonality above all---and act on them in the same
directions.
