\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The seven models differ in tokenization, depth and training objective, yet they select the same series for a concept,
share whole-layer geometry, and contain features with similar broad effects on the level and seasonality of the forecast.
The similarity largely ends at the level of individual causal effects. Fine-grained concepts are organized by model, the
same function can be carried by one head or by a five-head circuit, and the same effect on the same series is detected
only within one model family. One interpretation is that the models converge on which properties of a series are
predictive, which is largely determined by the data, while the way the forecast responds to them depends on the
architecture. This is a weaker form of convergence than that proposed for vision and language models
\citep{li2016convergent,huh2024platonic}. With the present instruments, agreement on inputs is measurable and replicates,
whereas agreement on effects across model families lies below the resolution of single-feature ablation at these
effect sizes.

The results also have practical consequences for interpreting TSFMs. Single dictionary features are only weakly
specific relative to directions the model actually uses, so single-feature claims, including those of recent
single-model SAE studies \citep{mishra2026dissecting,oublal2026timesae,divo2026zeitgeist}, should be evaluated against
an on-manifold null. Feature importance has to be established by intervention, since activation prominence, attention
and correlation with a generative factor each identify the wrong components. Cross-model comparisons need a measured
time axis and a pooling that preserves the order of time steps within a window. Finally, with thousands of exploratory
comparisons \citep{gelman2014garden}, registration and a single held-out test were essential. They confirmed between
half and two thirds of the registered claims (43 of 82 and 41 of 63), and the claim types separated consistently into
those that replicate and those that do not.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Models and alignment.} The study covers seven transformer models with contiguous tokens; models whose tokens
read disjoint lags, such as Lag-Llama, are out of scope. With seven models, differences between models are confounded
with architecture, size and training data, and Timer's features do not exceed chance, so its results carry no evidence
about causal concepts. The analysis is univariate (Chronos-2's cross-series attention is not measured; \fid{PM-13}), and
Chronos-T5's decoder and Sundial's flow head are not captured. The common time grid is no finer than the coarsest token,
and the locations of peak layer pairs, though not the size of the similarity, depend on the window size (\fid{SH-23}). Dictionaries are
trained on window vectors, which is conservative for one-step models (\fid{MP-11}).

\paragraph{Ground truth and scope.} Ground truth is exact only for the synthetic tier and encodes the generators'
assumptions, and overlap with the models' pretraining data cannot be verified. Raw real benchmarks are a weaker basis for
causal questions, because weakly predictable real series receive flat forecasts in 67--100\% of cases (\fid{BM-06};
cf.\ \citealp{wan2026collapse}), leaving an ablation nothing to move. Real data therefore enter as real-derived series
and as the external test set on which the transfer claims confirm. The confirmed transfers derive from two source
concepts on v2 and three source models on v3, so the shared-input result rests on few concepts. Concept results depend
on dictionary size, sparsity and seed; three replicate seeds measure this dependence but cannot remove it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We compared seven time-series foundation models on two independent corpora, using causal instruments validated on
planted concepts and testing registered claims once on sealed data. Architectures that differ substantially share
representational geometry, select the same inputs for a concept and contain features that raise or lower the level of
the forecast or damp its seasonality. Their causal structure, however, is model-specific and distributed. Several
practices adopted from language-model interpretability, including averaging tokens, ranking features by activation and
reading attention patterns, give misleading results on these models. The benchmark, the analysis software and the
reproduction commands for all results are public, so the conclusions can be re-tested on new models.
