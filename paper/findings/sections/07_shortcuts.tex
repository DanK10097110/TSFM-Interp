\section{Pitfalls in Transferring Interpretability Methods}
\label{sec:shortcuts}

Several practices that are standard in language-model interpretability give misleading results on TSFMs. In each case
below, the problem was identified by a control and would otherwise have gone unnoticed.

\paragraph{Window pooling.} As shown in Section~\ref{sec:geometry}, averaging the tokens within a window understates
TimesFM's held-out similarity to four fine-token models by 0.30--0.43 CKA. It also changes which models appear similar.
With mean-pooled representations, TimesFM/Chronos-2 ranks 15th of 21 pairs by CKA, although it is the most similar pair
by error agreement (0.948); with concatenated windows it ranks 9th on v2 and 7th on v3. Comparisons of TSFM
representations therefore depend on a pooling choice that is rarely reported.

\paragraph{Activation prominence.} SAE analyses usually start from the features that fire most often or most strongly.
In TSFMs these are mostly inert. Of the 927 most prominent features of the v2 private atlas, 0.835 have no causal effect
on the forecast (one-sided lower bound 0.817; dev 0.810; $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0005$; \fid{MN-14}), and on v3 the share is
0.919 of 861. Ranking by measured effect does much better. Zeroing a layer's five attribution-ranked atoms produces a
median 0.74 share of the forecast change the dictionary can express, more than activation-matched random sets of five
atoms on 30 of 31 targets (\fid{D-42C}).

\paragraph{Attention patterns.} The attention a head places on seasonal lags is uncorrelated with the seasonal power lost
when the head is ablated ($\rho=0.026$ in TimesFM and $-0.118$ in Chronos-T5-Base; \fid{CA-04}). This extends the
observation of \citet{jain2019attention} to forecasting models.

\paragraph{Correlation with generative factors.} Because the benchmark has exact ground truth, features can be matched
to generative factors. After dead-feature revival, 154 of 10{,}240 TimesFM features and 115 of 6{,}144 Chronos-T5-Base
features correlate with a generative field beyond a permutation null, most strongly with intermittency ($\rho=0.881$;
\fid{MP-06}). The correspondence is not specific, however. Varying the generator parameter along a dose ladder moves the
matched feature no more than random features of matched activation, and none of 151 registered tests survives
correction (\fid{MN-17}). In an earlier exploratory run, steering the feature best matched to seasonal amplitude changed
the forecast's seasonality in the expected direction in TimesFM and in the opposite direction in Chronos-T5-Base, in two
independently trained dictionaries per model (\fid{CA-05}).

\paragraph{Choice of null.} Whether a feature counts as causal depends on the random intervention it is compared with,
and reasonable choices differ severalfold. A null that removes one batch-averaged amount instead of each token's own
amount inflates the number of causal features 2.7-fold (110 to 293) and fails the planted-forecaster test on every seed
(Section~\ref{sec:knownanswer}). Between the two nulls that pass, isotropic directions and directions drawn from the
activation distribution, the excess over chance falls from 3.55-fold to 1.29-fold (Section~\ref{sec:distributed}).

\paragraph{Similarity metrics.} Seven pairwise similarity metrics, covering geometry, error agreement and concept
transfer, rank the 21 model pairs inconsistently (Kendall's $W=0.25$, $p=0.0095$; \fid{SH-05}, \fid{SH-06}). Geometry
also does not reflect lineage. Two untrained Chronos-T5 models are more similar (CKA 0.878) than the trained and related
Chronos-T5-Small and Base (0.734; \fid{MN-01}). We therefore report each metric against its own null and do not combine
them into a single score.
