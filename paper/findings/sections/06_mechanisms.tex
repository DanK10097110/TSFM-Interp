\section{Model-Specific Causal Structure}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

Shared inputs and similar broad effects do not imply that the models share mechanisms, that is, the same concept with
the same effect on the same series. At this level of detail we find little sharing.

\subsection{Concepts are organized by model}
\label{sec:bymodel}

The atlas groups causal features from all models whose effect profiles agree at cosine 0.9. It finds more structure than
a column-shuffle null (11 concepts, null 95th percentile 5.05; $p=0.005$), but of the 11 concepts, none spans more than 3
of the 7 models. The clusters are purer by model than random assignment makes them: 9 multi-model concepts against a
null mean of 10.8 ($p_{\text{below}}=0.025$), mean model purity 0.591 against 0.474 (\fid{SH-22}). Five of the 11 are
convergent, meaning that two models produce the same effect on different series. The pattern holds on held-out data, where no concept of the
private atlas spans more than 3 of 7 models on v2 or more than 2 on v3 (\fid{SH-16}). Each architecture appears to
build its own variants of level and seasonality effects.

A component-level example comes from seasonality. In an earlier two-model run, restoring a deseasonalized context
required a single attention head in TimesFM but a minimal set of five heads in Chronos-T5-Base, whose contributions do
not add (path-patching gap 0.0238
against a mean effect of 0.0108; \fid{CA-03}; exploratory).

\subsection{Agreement of causal effects}
\label{sec:sameeffect}

The strictest test of sharing ablates both sides of a reciprocal transfer on the same series. Of 1{,}290 tests, 216 are scorable
(Figure~\ref{fig:funnel}b): 7 show the same causal effect, 54 agree on level or shape alone, 133 show no specific
agreement and 22 act differently. All 7 ``same'' verdicts are between Chronos-2 and Chronos-Bolt, two models from one
family, six of them through one feature set in Chronos-Bolt's fourth block. The test produced no false
``same'' verdicts on the planted forecaster, so we regard these detections as reliable. It is also insensitive, so the
lack of detections across families does not by itself show that effects differ, although the 22 ``acts differently''
verdicts indicate that they sometimes do. Consistent with this, no registered cross-model causal claim confirms held-out. Three multi-model atlas concepts exceed the
null in centroid cosine on private data (0.955--0.988 against 95th percentiles 0.854--0.907) but fail the registered
member-pair rule, and 0 of 6 shared-input agreement claims confirm.

\subsection{Specificity of single features}
\label{sec:distributed}

\paragraph{Causal features.} Of 56 target layers, 55 pass both reach controls. Across them, 442 feature-channel
cells clear the isotropic null where the empirical chance rate predicts 178.75 (2.47 times chance), from 1.64 times
(Chronos-T5-Base) to 5.70 (Chronos-Bolt). Timer is the exception. Its layers reach the forecast, but its features clear
at 1.02 times chance (\fid{PM-17}). Target by target, 22 of 55 targets clear significantly more cells than chance, at
least two in every model except Timer. Registered single-feature effects replicate only in part. Of 31 testable claims, 10
confirm on the v2 private split, each at the Holm floor for 31 tests ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.031$), and 11 of 24 on v3.

\paragraph{Comparison with on-manifold directions.} The v3 run scored every feature against both nulls. Under the
isotropic null, 238 of 967 scorable features clear at least one channel; under the covariance-matched null, 91 do.
Scoring each null draw as a pseudo-feature on all nine channels at once gives expected counts of 67.0 and 70.4, so
features exceed chance 3.55-fold against isotropic directions ($p=4\times10^{-86}$) but only 1.29-fold against directions
drawn from the activation distribution ($p=0.0045$). Removing a typical on-manifold direction thus moves the forecast nearly
as much as removing a dictionary feature. The effects are also small. The median single-feature effect is 0.097 context
standard deviations, 2.2 times smaller than the 0.214 by which substituting the reconstruction moves the forecast (v3:
1.7 times; \fid{MN-15}).

\paragraph{Other interventions.} We re-tested the 91 v3 features
that clear the on-manifold null under three on-manifold interventions, each with its own matched null. These replace the
feature by its corpus mean, by its activation on another series where it fires, or subtract it from the model's own
activation rather than the reconstruction. Of the 91, 79 clear under at least one and 37 under all three, and mean and resample
ablation preserve the direction of the effect (median cosine to zero ablation 0.997 and 0.991; \fid{MP-10}). Most of
these features therefore have a genuine effect, but which of them clear depends on the intervention.

\paragraph{Absence of discrete units.} Within a layer, 50 of 56 targets are non-modular, meaning that no
choice of $k$ splits their effect vectors into clusters of at least three members each. The families of
Section~\ref{sec:families} cover 192 of 194 features but do not beat a column-shuffle null in number ($p=0.68$), and the
silhouette is 0.25--0.32 for every cut from 6 to 16 families. The effect vectors are not low-dimensional (participation
ratio 5.58 of 9 channels on v3; \fid{MN-07}), so the lack of clusters is not due to a lack of variation. Together with the
on-manifold comparison, this indicates that forecast-relevant information is spread over many directions and varies
continuously. This differs from the monosemantic units sought in language models
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity}, and it means that claims about single TSFM features should be tested against an
on-manifold null.
