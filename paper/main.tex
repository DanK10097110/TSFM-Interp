\begin{abstract}%
Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) are deployed zero-shot, yet the tools that made language models
inspectable do not transfer: tokens span different lengths of time across architectures, no ground truth
defines a forecasting ``concept'', and causal effects of internal features are small enough that uncontrolled
measurements produce confident false positives. We present \tool{}, an open-source system that turns a list of
checkpoints into one self-contained interpretability report with one command. It is controlled: a
leakage-audited synthetic benchmark with known generative components validates every probe. It is causal:
concepts are defined by what removing them does to the forecast, against matched random-direction nulls and
reach controls. It is confirmatory: exploratory claims are registered and tested once on a sealed split.
Applied to seven TSFMs (TimesFM, Chronos-2, Sundial, Chronos-Bolt, Chronos-T5, Timer and Time-MoE), it finds that
causal features mainly change forecast level and seasonality; that models agree on which inputs a concept groups
(20 of 20 registered claims confirmed on 971 held-out series) but that no cross-model claim about what a concept
does confirms there (multi-model concepts 0 of 3, shared-input agreement 0 of 6 tested); and that no concept spans
more than three of the seven models. In all, 43 of 82 replicable registered claims confirm: models share what they
select, not demonstrably what they do with it. A planted-concept forecaster showed our first ablation null to be
lenient, and we document fifteen plausible results that the system's own controls showed to be wrong.
\end{abstract}
