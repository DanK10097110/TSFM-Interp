\begin{abstract}%
Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) are used zero-shot, but the interpretability methods developed for language
models do not transfer to them directly. Tokens cover different spans of time in different architectures, real series
offer no ground truth for the concepts a probe might find, and the effects of single internal features on a forecast are
small enough that uncontrolled measurements yield confident false positives. We present \tool{}, an open-source system
that compares TSFMs on a measured common time axis, validates its instruments on a benchmark with known generative
components and on a forecaster with planted concepts, defines concepts by their effect on the forecast relative to
matched random interventions, and tests exploratory claims once on a sealed held-out split. We apply it to seven TSFMs
and confirm the results on two independent corpora. Different architectures select the same input series for a
concept: all registered transfer claims confirm on both private splits and on 800 raw real-world windows. Causal
features act mainly on forecast level and seasonality, but fine-grained concepts are organized by model, and no concept
spans more than three of the seven models. Activation prominence is a poor guide to causal importance, since 83.5\% of
prominent features have no held-out effect, and fewer than half of single-feature causal claims replicate. Against
random directions drawn from the activation distribution, single features are at most weakly specific, and neither
more data, a more powerful test nor gradient-selected candidates changes this. Every claim is reported with its
evidence class and a reproduction pointer.
\end{abstract}
