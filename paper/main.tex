\title{\tool: Controlled, Causal and Confirmatory\\ Interpretability for Time-Series Foundation Models}

\author{\name Daniel Kushnir \AND \name Hao Wang}

\editor{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}%
Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) are now deployed zero-shot in forecasting pipelines, yet
almost nothing is known about what they compute, and the tools that made language models
inspectable do not transfer: TSFM tokens cover different spans of time from one architecture to the
next, no ground truth exists for what a ``concept'' in a forecast should be, and the causal effects
of internal features are small enough that uncontrolled measurements produce confident false
positives. We present \tool{}, an open-source system that turns a list of model checkpoints into a
single, self-contained interpretability report with one command. It is built on three commitments.
First, it is controlled: a leakage-audited synthetic benchmark whose generative components
are known exactly, together with structured input perturbations, provides known answers against
which every probe is validated. Second, it is causal: concepts are defined by what removing
them does to the forecast, measured against row-matched random-direction nulls and gated by reach
controls. Third, it is confirmatory: exploratory claims are registered by content hash and
tested once on a sealed private split. Architecture-agnostic time alignment makes decoder-only,
encoder-only and encoder--decoder models with token widths from 1 to 96 steps comparable, and every
reported number carries a typed evidence class and a generated caveat. Applied to TimesFM,
Chronos-2, Sundial and Chronos-Bolt, the system finds that the models' causal features change
forecast level and shape far more often than trend or seasonality; that the models agree on
which inputs a concept groups (20 of 20 registered transfer claims confirmed on 800 held-out
series) but rarely on what it does to those inputs (11 of 1{,}223 shared-input tests show the
same causal effect); and that no concept is shared by all four models. We also document
more than a dozen cases in which a plausible, well-formed result was wrong and the system's own
controls caught it. Code, configurations, a complete example run and a ledger of every finding with
its reproduction pointer are public.
\end{abstract}
