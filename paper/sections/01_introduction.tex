\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Pretrained forecasting models are now default components. TimesFM \citep{das2024timesfm}, the Chronos
family \citep{ansari2024chronos,ansari2025chronos2}, Sundial \citep{liu2025sundial}, Timer
\citep{liu2024timer}, Moirai \citep{woo2024moirai}, MOMENT \citep{goswami2024moment}, Lag-Llama
\citep{rasul2023lagllama} and Time-MoE \citep{shi2025timemoe} are released as open checkpoints and used
zero-shot. Their architectures differ sharply: some are decoder-only stacks over 32-step patches, others are
encoder--decoders over quantized single steps, encoders with cross-series attention, or decoders with sampled
flow-matching heads. Leaderboards say which of them is more accurate. They do not say what these models represent,
whether architectures this different represent the same things, or whether any internal structure we can name
drives the forecast.

Mechanistic interpretability offers the instruments to ask: probes \citep{alain2017probes,hewitt2019control},
representational similarity \citep{kornblith2019cka}, activation patching
\citep{vig2020causal,meng2022rome,zhang2024patching}, lenses \citep{belrose2023tuned} and sparse dictionaries
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,gao2025topk}, packaged in libraries such as TransformerLens
\citep{nanda2022transformerlens} and pyvene \citep{wu2024pyvene}. Early work applies pieces of this toolkit to single
TSFMs \citep{wilinski2025exploring,mishra2026dissecting,oublal2026timesae,divo2026zeitgeist}. Three properties of
forecasting models prevent a direct transfer, and each produces silent errors rather than crashes.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Tokens are not comparable across models.} One token covers 1 time step in Chronos-T5, 16 in
    Chronos-2, 32 in TimesFM and 96 in Timer. A cross-model comparison therefore needs a verified mapping from
    tokens to time.
  \item \textbf{There is no ground truth for concepts.} Real series carry no labels for trend, period,
    changepoints or noise process, so a probe that ``finds seasonality'' cannot be checked on them.
  \item \textbf{Effects are small and easy to fake.} Removing one sparse feature typically moves a forecast by
    about a tenth of the context's standard deviation, less than the dictionary's own reconstruction error. At
    this scale a precision mismatch, an intervention the forecast head never reads, or a null measured on the
    wrong rows each produce a clean, confident and wrong result.
\end{enumerate}

We present \tool{}, a system built around these three problems, and use it to study four research questions:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,font=\normalfont\bfseries,itemsep=1pt]
  \item[RQ1.] Which forecast-relevant concepts do TSFMs learn?
  \item[RQ2.] Are these concepts shared across architectures, in the sense of responding to the same inputs,
    having the same effect, or both?
  \item[RQ3.] Do the concepts causally affect the forecast, and can their importance be read from activations?
  \item[RQ4.] Where in depth do these computations happen, and how comparable is depth across architectures?
\end{description}

The system makes three methodological commitments. It is \emph{controlled}: a synthetic benchmark with exact
generative ground truth, and a forecaster with planted concepts, give every probe a case with a known answer. It is
\emph{causal}: a feature counts as a concept only if removing it changes the forecast more than removing a random
direction of matched size on the same series, and reach controls show that the intervention landed. It is
\emph{confirmatory}: findings on the development (dev) split are frozen in a hash-pinned registry and tested once on
a sealed private split. Measured \emph{time alignment} makes architectures comparable, and the output is a single
report in which every claim states its evidence class.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item \textbf{A comparative interpretability system} (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}): measured token-to-time
    alignment, a zero-code adapter path for new checkpoints, and a gated pipeline that turns a list of checkpoints
    into one self-contained report, together with a benchmark generator with leakage-audited tiers and sealed,
    bit-exactly regenerable splits.
  \item \textbf{A causal concept methodology} (Section~\ref{sec:methods}) that defines concepts by their effect on
    the forecast and separates two notions of sharing that are usually conflated: \emph{same inputs} and \emph{same
    effect on the same inputs}.
  \item \textbf{Known-answer validation of the instruments} (Section~\ref{sec:knownanswer}): every null and
    agreement test is held to a pass criterion fixed in advance on a forecaster with planted concepts and decoys,
    which rejected two plausible designs.
  \item \textbf{A seven-model study with one-shot confirmation on two corpora} (Sections~\ref{sec:setup}
    and~\ref{sec:casestudy}), with a statistical protocol of series-level resampling, a multiplicity ledger and
    registered hypotheses (Section~\ref{sec:statistics}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Main findings.} We study TimesFM~2.5, Chronos-2, Sundial, Chronos-Bolt, Chronos-T5-Base, Timer and
Time-MoE, and re-test the registered claims on a second, three times larger corpus.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1.} Sparse-dictionary features whose removal moves the forecast more than a random direction of
    the same size exist in six of the seven models. They act mostly on forecast level and seasonality and form a
    graded continuum rather than discrete units. Against random directions drawn from the layer's own activation
    distribution, however, they exceed chance only 1.29-fold. The effects are not an artifact of ablating to zero:
    most survive mean ablation.
  \item \textbf{RQ2.} Representational geometry is shared above chance for all 21 model pairs, which a registered
    test confirms on a fresh sealed split, provided windows concatenate their tokens: averaging them, the common
    practice, hides 0.30--0.43 of TimesFM's CKA with the fine-token models. Different architectures also \emph{select the
    same series} for a concept: all registered transfer claims confirm held-out on both corpora and on raw real-world
    windows. The same broad effect types (raising or
    lowering the level, damping seasonality) recur across models, but fine-grained concepts are organized by model:
    no concept spans more than three of the seven models, which also holds held-out.
  \item \textbf{RQ3.} Activation prominence does not identify causal importance: most prominent features have no
    held-out causal effect. Fewer than half of the registered single-feature causal claims replicate, and neither
    more data, a more powerful test nor gradient-selected candidates improves this.
  \item \textbf{RQ4.} How TimesFM and Chronos-2 react to corruptions, layer by layer, replicates held-out for 6 of 9
    corruptions on v2 and 4 of 9 on v3 (8 of 9 keep their sign), with level shift flipping sign on both; absolute depth locations are comparable only after accounting for how much of each model's compute
    is captured.
\end{itemize}
