\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Pretrained forecasting models are now default components. TimesFM \citep{das2024timesfm}, the Chronos
family \citep{ansari2024chronos,ansari2025chronos2}, Sundial \citep{liu2025sundial}, Timer
\citep{liu2024timer}, Moirai \citep{woo2024moirai}, MOMENT \citep{goswami2024moment}, Lag-Llama
\citep{rasul2023lagllama} and Time-MoE \citep{shi2025timemoe} are released as open checkpoints and used
zero-shot. Their architectures differ sharply (decoder-only stacks over 32-step patches,
encoder--decoders over quantized single steps, encoders with cross-series attention, sampled
flow-matching heads), and leaderboards say which is more accurate. They do not say what these models
represent, whether architectures this different represent the same things, or whether any internal
structure we can name drives the forecast.

Mechanistic interpretability offers probes \citep{alain2017probes,hewitt2019control}, representational
similarity \citep{kornblith2019cka}, activation patching \citep{vig2020causal,meng2022rome,zhang2024patching},
lenses \citep{belrose2023tuned} and sparse dictionaries \citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,gao2025topk},
packaged in libraries such as TransformerLens \citep{nanda2022transformerlens} and pyvene
\citep{wu2024pyvene}, and early work applies pieces of this to TSFMs
\citep{wilinski2025exploring,mishra2026dissecting,oublal2026timesae,divo2026zeitgeist}. Three properties
of forecasting models break the direct transfer, and each causes silent errors rather than crashes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Tokens are not comparable across models.} One token is 1 time step in Chronos-T5, 32 in
    TimesFM, 16 in Chronos-2 and 96 in Timer, so every cross-model comparison needs a verified mapping
    from tokens to time.
  \item \textbf{There is no ground truth for concepts.} Real series carry no labels for trend, period,
    changepoints or noise process, so a probe that ``finds seasonality'' cannot be checked.
  \item \textbf{Effects are small and easy to fake.} Removing one sparse feature typically moves a forecast
    by a few hundredths of the context's standard deviation, less than the dictionary's own reconstruction
    error. At that scale a precision mismatch, a patch the head never reads or a null measured on the wrong
    rows yields a clean, confident and wrong result.
\end{enumerate}

We present \tool{}, a system designed around these problems, and use it to study three questions:
\textbf{RQ1}, what forecast-relevant concepts TSFMs learn; \textbf{RQ2}, whether these concepts are shared
across architectures, in the sense of responding to the same inputs, having the same effect, or both; and
\textbf{RQ3}, whether they causally affect the forecast and how much of it they account for. A supporting
question, \textbf{RQ4}, asks where in depth these computations happen and how comparable depth is across
architectures.

\tool{} rests on three commitments. It is \emph{controlled}: a synthetic benchmark with exact generative
ground truth and a battery of structured perturbations gives every probe a known answer. It is
\emph{causal}: a feature counts only if removing it changes the forecast more than removing a random
direction of the same size on the same series, with reach controls proving the intervention landed. It is
\emph{confirmatory}: dev findings are frozen in a hash-pinned registry and tested once on a sealed private
split. Measured \emph{time alignment} makes architectures comparable, and a \emph{one-command report}
explains every figure in plain language.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item An open-source system (${\sim}$61k lines, 171 test modules) that turns a configuration listing
    checkpoints into a self-contained HTML report through 19 fingerprinted stages, with a zero-code adapter
    path (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}).
  \item A controlled benchmark generator with two leakage tiers, a leakage audit, sealed splits with fresh
    epochs and bit-exact regeneration (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).
  \item A causal concept pipeline that separates two notions of sharing that are usually conflated:
    \emph{same inputs} and \emph{same effect on the same inputs} (Section~\ref{sec:concepts}).
  \item A statistical protocol with series-level resampling, a multiplicity ledger and one-shot
    confirmation (Section~\ref{sec:statistics}).
  \item A four-model study answering RQ1--RQ4 with typed evidence and held-out replication
    (Section~\ref{sec:casestudy}), and a catalogue of fourteen cases in which an uncontrolled analysis gave
    a plausible, wrong answer that a built-in control caught (Section~\ref{sec:controls}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Main findings.} On TimesFM~2.5, Chronos-2, Sundial and Chronos-Bolt: (RQ1) causal sparse
features exist (293 across 29 layers clear a row-matched null) but act mostly on forecast \emph{level}
and \emph{shape}, and tile a continuum rather than forming discrete concepts; (RQ2) models agree on
\emph{which inputs} a concept groups (20 of 20 registered transfer claims confirmed on 800 sealed series)
but rarely on \emph{what it does} to them (11 of 1{,}223 shared-input tests show the same causal effect),
and no concept is shared by all four models; (RQ3) single features are causal but small, and 64\% of
visually prominent features have no causal effect; (RQ4) depth-resolved reaction profiles replicate
held-out (9 of 9 corruptions), but depth locations are comparable only after accounting for captured
compute (14\% to 98\% of forecast FLOPs).

The full ledger of results, each with exact numbers, evidence class, status and a reproduction pointer,
is \repo{FINDINGS.md} (indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}). The example run of
Section~\ref{sec:casestudy}, with its report and every per-stage artifact, is
\repodir{examples/concept_atlas_v2}; \repo{paper/figures/make_figures.py} regenerates every data figure
from it.
