\section{Case Study: Four Time-Series Foundation Models}
\label{sec:casestudy}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We analyze TimesFM~2.5, Chronos-2, Sundial and Chronos-Bolt with the preset
\repo{tsfm_lens/configs/concept_atlas_v2.yaml}. The dev corpus has 965 series in five generator families; the
real-derived ones draw on Monash weather, ETT \citep{zhou2021informer} and the Chronos electricity and traffic
collections. Contexts are 512 steps, the horizon 64, the window 32. SAEs are trained on 29 target layers
(TimesFM 10, Chronos-2 7, Sundial 9, Chronos-Bolt 3) with three replicate seeds each, and
$n_{\text{boot}}=2000$. The private split is 800 sealed series, opened once. The run took 6.43 hours on one
RTX A5000 (24~GB). Artifacts are in \repodir{examples/concept_atlas_v2/run}; results from other runs carry
their \repo{FINDINGS.md} identifier.

Table~\ref{tab:character} gives the models' behavior. Chronos-2 is most accurate on dev and held-out data;
Sundial's nominal 80\% interval covers 37\% of outcomes; TimesFM and Sundial propagate an injected NaN
silently. The Sundial row hides a bug found in this work: the first adapter bypassed the checkpoint's input
normalization, and a scale-equivariance residual of 38.43 context standard deviations was at first read as
a model property (\fid{PM-15}, Appendix~\ref{app:adapters}; after the fix, 0.033).

\subsection{RQ1: What forecast-relevant concepts do TSFMs learn?}
\label{sec:rq1}

\paragraph{Causal features exist, and the battery discriminates.} 293 SAE features across 29 targets clear the
row-matched null on at least one channel. Twenty-eight targets passed both reach controls (e.g.\ TimesFM
\texttt{stacked\_xf.10}: self-patch 0.0, cross-patch reach 0.146). Chronos-2's final block was withheld:
cross-patching moved the forecast by exactly 0, because its head reads placeholder tokens a context patch never
reaches (\fid{DE-03}); without the probe it would have reported a table of zero effects. On the reference run,
(feature, channel) cells clear the null at 2.49--6.61 times the chance rate, and randomly nominated features
clear at about half the rate of activation-nominated ones (\fid{MP-07}).

\paragraph{The concepts are about level and shape.} Nine effect families tile the causal features
(Figure~\ref{fig:families}). The largest act on level and volatility: \emph{Level lowerers} (80), \emph{Level
raisers \& Accuracy improvers} (61), \emph{Volatility dampeners} (48). Trend and seasonality families are
smaller (23, 18, 17 and 12 members), and nearly every family also moves the shape distances. Level is not
everything: the median pure-level share of a causal effect is 0.535, and most features still clear a null
after the level shift is removed from both feature and null (\fid{CA-07}).

\paragraph{The concepts are graded, not discrete.} Within a layer, 24 of 29 targets are non-modular (9
per-target concepts in total); the strict atlas assigns only 86 of 293 features to its 27 concepts; and the
families cover 288 of 293 features but do \emph{not} beat a column-shuffle null ($p=1.0$ for their number,
$0.985$ for coverage). On the reference run silhouette is 0.31--0.35 for every family count from 3 to 16
(\fid{CA-10}) and the ablation fingerprints have participation ratio 2.09 (\fid{MN-07}). Families are thus a
reading vocabulary, not discovered units.

\paragraph{Ground-truth concepts are rare and need a null.} After dead-feature revival, 154 of 10{,}240
TimesFM and 115 of 6{,}144 Chronos-T5-Base features match a ground-truth field beyond a permutation null (best:
intermittency, $\rho=0.881$; \fid{MP-06}); raw correlations do not separate trained from untrained weights
(0.567 vs.\ 0.565). Validating concepts by moving a generator knob was a confirmed negative twice (0 of 98,
then 0 of 151 pairs), because a knob moves any active feature about as much as the targeted one (\fid{MN-17}),
and a vivid TimesFM ``random walk'' concept failed seed stability (\fid{PM-11}).

\paragraph{Answer to RQ1.} TSFMs contain sparse causal directions that mainly control forecast level,
volatility and shape, with trend and seasonality in smaller groups. They form a continuum with a few dominant
axes, not discrete concepts aligned with named generative factors.

\subsection{RQ2: Are concepts shared across architectures?}
\label{sec:rq2}

\paragraph{Geometry is shared above chance, but similarity has no single meaning.} Peak CKA between TimesFM and
each other model is 0.38--0.43 (null 0.037); the TimesFM/Chronos-2 peak replicates held-out (dev 0.4262;
private 0.4253 [0.4162, 0.4423]), and the band holds across five architectures including Timer and Lag-Llama
(\fid{SH-01}). Yet seven pair metrics rank the six pairs inconsistently (Kendall's $W=0.28$, $p=0.07$;
Figure~\ref{fig:similarity}): TimesFM/Chronos-2 is first by error agreement (0.947) and fourth by CKA, so the
metrics are never pooled. An untrained same-architecture pair is more CKA-similar (0.878) than a related trained
pair (0.734, $p=0.0005$; \fid{MN-01}), and a unit-correspondence measure \emph{rose} under random rotation (0.148
to 0.331) because the layers are so low-rank (\fid{SH-19}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{figures/similarity.pdf}
\caption{Rank of each model pair (columns) under seven similarity metrics (rows); 1 is most similar. Source:
\texttt{report/model\_similarity.json}.}
\label{fig:similarity}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Models agree on which inputs a concept groups.} 1{,}223 of 1{,}581 atlas transfer tests are
reciprocal after BH correction. Twenty claims registered from dev alone were all confirmed on the 800 private
series (Holm, maximum adjusted $p=0.0100$; AUC 0.971--0.995 against null 95th percentiles of 0.57--0.77;
Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}b), replicating on three independent private draws (\fid{SH-18}). This is an
input-level claim, and the 20 claims come from two concepts with only 6 of 20 private best features identical
to dev: the destination has \emph{some} feature selecting the same series.

\paragraph{Models rarely agree on what a concept does.} Of 27 atlas concepts, 16 have causal members from
$\ge$2 models, but only 2 are \emph{shared}, 1 partially shared, 13 \emph{convergent} (same effect, different
series) and 11 single-model (Figure~\ref{fig:funnel}); among the 22 seed-stable concepts the counts are 2, 1, 9
and 10. None spans all four models. Of the 1{,}223 reciprocal transfers, 420 are scorable, and only 11 (2.6\%)
show the same causal effect; 81 agree on level or shape alone, 264 show no specific agreement and 64 act
differently.

\centering

\caption{(a) Concept candidates at each rung (first three bars in sequence; last three are properties of the 27
atlas concepts). (b) Shared-input causal agreement over 1{,}223 reciprocal transfers. Source:

\label{fig:funnel}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.} Different TSFMs \emph{detect} the same things reliably enough to replicate on sealed
data, but mostly do not \emph{use} them the same way: the typical multi-model concept is convergent, and none is
common to all four models. Whole-layer similarity cannot reveal this and is internally inconsistent.

\subsection{RQ3: Do concepts causally affect the forecast, and how much?}
\label{sec:rq3}

Individually, little. The median single-feature effect is 0.0395 context standard deviations, 3.5 times below
the 0.139 moved by merely substituting the SAE reconstruction (\fid{MN-15}). Of 565 strongly activating
``interesting'' features, 364 (64.4\%) clear no causal channel (\fid{MN-14}), and \tool{} collapses them
rather than rendering them. Dead-atom revival improved fidelity yet lowered ground-truth alignment at every
TimesFM depth tested ($-0.030$ to $-0.132$; \fid{MN-21}). At the head level, attention mass on seasonal lags is
uncorrelated with the seasonal power lost when the head is ablated ($\rho=0.026$ TimesFM, $-0.118$
Chronos-T5-Base; \fid{CA-04}), consistent with \citet{jain2019attention}; a minimal seasonality circuit is one
head in TimesFM but five non-additive heads in Chronos-T5-Base (\fid{CA-03}).

\paragraph{Answer to RQ3.} Features and heads are causal in the controlled sense, but contributions are small
and distributed, most prominent features carry nothing, and attention is an unreliable guide.

\subsection{RQ4: Where in depth, and how comparable is depth?}
\label{sec:rq4}

For TimesFM/Chronos-2 all nine per-corruption fingerprint agreements replicate held-out (overall
$\rho=0.510$ [0.489, 0.527]; Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}a): the models react at matching depths to detrending,
deseasonalizing, frequency shift and dropout, and at opposite depths to noise ($-0.742$), spikes ($-0.780$),
warps ($-0.658$) and smoothing ($-0.466$). In TimesFM, whole-layer restoration is flat across depth but
per-window restoration concentrates just before the horizon and grows with depth (\texttt{noise}: 0.068 at
layer 0 to 0.412 at layer 16; \fid{DE-01}). Depth \emph{locations} need two qualifications that every report
renders: captured compute ranges from 14\% (Chronos-T5-Base) to 98\% (Chronos-2) of a forecast (\fid{DE-05}),
and Chronos-T5's last encoder block sits at 0.478 on the whole-stack axis (\fid{DE-04}). No selected layer was
robust across three layer selectors (\fid{DE-08}).

\paragraph{Answer to RQ4.} Depth-resolved reaction profiles are reproducible; absolute depth locations are
comparable only after accounting for captured compute and the depth convention.

\label{sec:heldout}

Of 47 registered hypotheses, 31 are currently re-testable, and every one held (Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}): the
accuracy claim (Chronos-2 better on \texttt{mixture}, $\Delta$MASE 0.307 [0.209, 0.413], $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0005$,
$n=240$; minimum detectable effect 0.155), the peak-CKA pair, all nine perturbation agreements and all 20
transfer claims (Table~\ref{tab:results}). The causal concept results of RQ1--RQ3 are exploratory, but reproduce
on a second corpus: on the v1 reference run no atlas concept spans four models, convergent is again modal, and
12 of 89 scorable shared-input tests act differently (\fid{SH-16}, \fid{CA-06}).
