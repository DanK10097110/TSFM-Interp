\section{Case Study: Seven Time-Series Foundation Models}
\label{sec:casestudy}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We analyze seven models with the preset \repo{tsfm_lens/configs/panel7_v2.yaml}: TimesFM~2.5 \citep{das2024timesfm},
Chronos-2 \citep{ansari2025chronos2}, Sundial \citep{liu2025sundial}, Chronos-Bolt and Chronos-T5-Base
\citep{ansari2024chronos}, Timer \citep{liu2024timer} and Time-MoE \citep{shi2025timemoe}; the last two load through the
zero-code \texttt{generic\_hf} adapter. The dev corpus has 965 series in five generator families; the real-derived ones
draw on Monash weather, ETT \citep{zhou2021informer} and the Chronos electricity and traffic collections. Contexts are
480 steps (a multiple of Timer's 96-step token), the horizon 64, the window 32. SAEs are trained on 56 target layers
(TimesFM 10, Chronos-2 9, Sundial 9, Chronos-Bolt 4, Timer 6, Time-MoE 9, Chronos-T5-Base 9) with three replicate seeds
each, and $n_{\text{boot}}=5000$. The private split is 971 sealed series (v2 epoch 3), opened once; its provenance is in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. The run recorded 21.1 hours of stage time on one RTX A5000 (24~GB). Artifacts are in
\repodir{examples/panel7_v2/run}. Ledger-tagged numbers measured in another run say so (the earlier four-model run is
\repodir{examples/concept_atlas_v2}); every other number is from this run or its confirmation.

Table~\ref{tab:character} gives the models' behavior. Chronos-2 is the most accurate; Time-MoE is less accurate than the
naive forecast and Timer close to the seasonal naive; Sundial's and Chronos-T5-Base's nominal 80\% intervals cover 36\%
and 44\% of outcomes; and TimesFM, Sundial, Timer and Time-MoE propagate an injected NaN silently.

\subsection{RQ1: What forecast-relevant concepts do TSFMs learn?}
\label{sec:rq1}

\paragraph{Causal features exist, and the battery tells models apart.} 194 SAE features
across 55 measured targets clear the profile-matched null on at least one channel (against at most 144.95 expected
by chance at the feature level, an upper bound that treats channels as independent). Fifty-five of 56 targets passed both
reach controls (e.g.\ TimesFM \texttt{stacked\_xf.10}: self-patch 0.0, cross-patch relative reach 0.253); Chronos-2's
final block was withheld because cross-patching moved the forecast by exactly 0 (its head reads placeholder tokens a
context patch never reaches; \fid{DE-03}). Against the leave-one-draw-out chance rate, 442 (feature, channel) cells clear
the null where 178.75 are expected (2.47 times chance), from 1.02 times (Timer) to 5.70 times (Chronos-Bolt); 22 of 55
targets are individually significant (BH at 0.05) and Timer has none, although its layers reach the forecast (relative
reach 0.066--0.261; \fid{PM-17}).

\paragraph{The concepts are about level and seasonality.} Nine effect families tile the causal features
(Figure~\ref{fig:families}). The two largest act on level: \emph{Level raisers} (45) and \emph{Level lowerers} (44),
89 of the 192 assigned features. Seasonality dampeners and amplifiers follow (25 and 19); three mixed-effect families
(14, 13, 11), trend boosters (11) and volatility dampeners (10) are smaller. Level is not everything: on the earlier four-model run (original null, an upper bound) the median pure-level share of
an effect was 0.535 (\fid{CA-07}).

\paragraph{The concepts are graded, not discrete.} Within a layer, 50 of 56 targets are non-modular (10 per-target
concepts in total, on 5 targets, 4 of them on Timer targets whose clears are not above chance); the strict atlas assigns
only 35 of 194 features to its 11 concepts, more than a column-shuffle null produces (null 95th percentile 5.05 concepts,
assigned fraction 0.088 against 0.180; $p=0.005$) but few; and the families cover 192 of 194 features but do \emph{not}
beat the shuffle null ($p=0.68$ for their number, $0.23$ for coverage). Silhouette is 0.25--0.32 for every cut from 6 to
16 families, and on the earlier run the ablation fingerprints had participation ratio 2.09 (\fid{CA-10}, \fid{MN-07}).
Families are thus a reading vocabulary, not discovered units.

\paragraph{Some features align with named generative factors.} After dead-feature revival, 154 of 10{,}240
TimesFM and 115 of 6{,}144 Chronos-T5-Base features match a ground-truth field beyond a permutation null (best:
intermittency, $\rho=0.881$; \fid{MP-06}). The alignment is correlational: moving one generator knob does not move a
matched feature's forecast effect beyond chance (\fid{MN-17}), and seed-stability checks filter vivid but unstable
candidates (\fid{PM-11}).

\paragraph{Answer to RQ1.} TSFMs learn a compact vocabulary of causal effects: sparse directions that control forecast
level and seasonality, and in smaller groups trend, volatility and mixtures. These effects form a continuum with a few
dominant axes rather than discrete units, which is why \tool{} reports families as a reading vocabulary. The battery
discriminates between models, from 5.70 times chance in Chronos-Bolt to chance level in Timer on this corpus.

\subsection{RQ2: Are concepts shared across architectures?}
\label{sec:rq2}

\paragraph{Geometry is shared above chance, but similarity has no single meaning.} Peak CKA (the maximum over all layer pairs) between
TimesFM and each other model is 0.342--0.426 (shuffled-series nulls 0.011--0.051); the TimesFM/Chronos-2 peak replicates held-out (dev
0.4257; private 0.4109 [0.4014, 0.4266]), and the band holds across five architectures including Timer and Lag-Llama
(\fid{SH-01}). Yet seven pair metrics rank the 21 pairs only weakly consistently (Kendall's $W=0.25$, $p=0.0095$;
Figure~\ref{fig:similarity}): TimesFM/Chronos-2 is first by error agreement (0.948) and 15th of 21 by CKA, where
Sundial/Chronos-Bolt is first (0.927), so the metrics are never pooled. Two finer-grained measures do not pass their controls on real activations (an untrained pair scores higher than a
trained related pair, \fid{MN-01}; a unit-correspondence measure rises under rotation, \fid{SH-19}), so the report
uses CKA against shuffled-series nulls and never reads it as lineage.

Chronos-Bolt, Tim Timer, MoE Time-MoE, T5 Chronos-T5-Base) under seven similarity metrics (rows); 1 is most similar.
Source: \texttt{report/model\_similarity.json}.}
\label{fig:similarity}

\paragraph{Models agree on which inputs a concept groups.} 1{,}296 of 1{,}584 atlas transfer tests are
reciprocal after BH correction. Twenty claims registered from dev alone were all confirmed on the 971 private
series (Holm, maximum adjusted $p=0.0100$, the floor for 20 claims at 2000 draws; AUC 0.919--0.970 against null 95th
percentiles of 0.70--0.75; Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}b), replicating on four private draws (19/20, 20/20, 20/20 on
earlier draws, and 20/20 here; \fid{SH-18}, \fid{CA-11}). This is an input-level claim. The 20 claims come from two
concepts (TimesFM \texttt{xf.18}, 14 claims; Sundial \texttt{layers.6}, 6) and reach destinations in all seven models,
with only 8 of 20 private best features identical to dev: the destination has \emph{some} feature selecting the same
series.

\paragraph{The basic effect types are universal.} The three largest families are found in every model: \emph{Level
raisers} (45 features), \emph{Level lowerers} (44) and \emph{Seasonality dampeners} (25) each have causal members in all
seven models, and \emph{Volatility dampeners} in six (Figure~\ref{fig:families}b). Membership is a property of each
feature's own measured effect, so it does not depend on whether the family partition beats its shuffle null; it is
descriptive and exploratory, and Timer's members should be read with its chance-level clear rate in mind.

\paragraph{Fine-grained concepts are organized by model.} Of 11 atlas concepts, 9 have causal members from $\ge$2 models,
but no concept spans more than 3 of the 7 models, and the clusters are \emph{purer} by model than random assignment: 9
multi-model concepts against a null mean of 10.8 ($p_{\text{below}}=0.025$) and mean model purity 0.591 against 0.474
(\fid{SH-22}). By sharing class, 2 concepts are \emph{shared} (same effect and inputs), 2 partially shared, 5
\emph{convergent} (same effect reached on different series) and 2 single-model (Figure~\ref{fig:funnel}); among the 9
seed-stable concepts the counts are 2, 1, 4 and 2. At this grain (cosine 0.9 between null-normalized effect
vectors) each architecture builds its own variants, and several arrive at the same effect independently.

\paragraph{Where the same effect is measured on the same series, it is detected within the Chronos family.} The
shared-input test ablates both sides of a reciprocal transfer on the same series and requires each to clear its own null.
Of 1{,}290 tests (a cap of 60 per ordered pair), 216 are scorable: 7 show the same causal effect, 54 agree on level or
shape alone, 133 show no specific agreement and 22 act differently. All 7 ``same'' verdicts are Chronos-2/Chronos-Bolt,
six through one Chronos-Bolt block-4 feature set. The test is specific (it never calls a planted decoy ``same''), so these
detections stand; it is also insensitive across architectures (\fid{MN-30}), and 1{,}053 of the 1{,}074 unscorable
tests fail because a side's own ablation does not clear its null on its 21--40 shared series (median 32), so we do not read the
absence of ``same'' elsewhere as a difference.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.} Different TSFMs \emph{select} the same series for a concept, reliably enough to replicate on
four sealed private draws and in all seven models. The basic effect types (raising or lowering the level, damping
seasonality) are present in every model, and the centroid effect profiles of multi-model concepts replicate held-out
(Section~\ref{sec:heldout}). At the fine grain, concepts are organized by model: no strict concept is common to the panel
(confirmed held-out), several converge on the same effect from different series, and the clearest
same-effect-on-same-inputs case is within the Chronos family. Whether architectures from different families share effects series by series is not measurable by
ablation at these effect sizes (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Whole-layer
similarity cannot reveal any of this, since its metrics disagree with one another.

\subsection{RQ3: Do concepts causally affect the forecast, and how much?}
\label{sec:rq3}

Causal importance is concentrated in a minority of features, and identifying that minority requires the battery.
Of the 32 single-feature causal claims registered from dev, 31 were testable on the private split and 10 confirm (TimesFM 3 of 4, Time-MoE 2 of 7, Sundial 2 of 4, Chronos-Bolt 2 of 6, Chronos-2 1 of 7,
Chronos-T5-Base 0 of 4; by channel, near-horizon shape 4 of 8, MASE 3 of 8, spectral centroid 2 of 4, dispersion 1 of 3,
seasonal 0 of 4, level 0 of 4); each confirmed claim has $p=0.001$ after an adaptive redraw to 1000 null draws and
$p_{\text{Holm}}=0.031$, the floor for 31 tests. The other 21 are not detected on the new series (19 of them
had not reached the 200-draw $p$ floor on dev; one flips sign, Chronos-T5-Base block 10). Activation prominence does not
identify the causal minority: 0.835 of the 927 prominent features of the private atlas are causally null (one-sided lower
bound 0.817, dev 0.810, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0005$; \fid{MN-14}), so \tool{} ranks features by measured effect rather than
by activation. On the earlier four-model run the median single-feature effect
was 0.0395 context standard deviations, 3.5 times below the 0.139 moved by substituting the SAE reconstruction
(\fid{MN-15}). Attention
mass on seasonal lags is uncorrelated with the seasonal power lost when the head is ablated ($\rho=0.026$ TimesFM,
$-0.118$ Chronos-T5-Base; \fid{CA-04}; cf.\ \citealt{jain2019attention})
(a minimal seasonality circuit is one head in TimesFM, five non-additive heads in Chronos-T5-Base; \fid{CA-03}).

\paragraph{Answer to RQ3.} Features and heads causally shape the forecast, and the strongest single-feature effects
replicate on new series at the attainable significance floor. Contributions are small and distributed, and neither
activation prominence nor attention mass locates them; a causal battery does.

\subsection{RQ4: Where in depth, and how comparable is depth?}
\label{sec:rq4}

For TimesFM/Chronos-2, 6 of the 9 per-corruption fingerprint agreements replicate held-out (overall $\rho=0.524$
[0.504, 0.544]; Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}a): the models react at matching depths to detrending (private 0.368), dropout
(0.510) and, more strongly than on dev, frequency shift (0.381 against 0.216), and at opposite depths to noise
($-0.743$), spikes ($-0.785$), warps ($-0.651$) and smoothing ($-0.581$ against $-0.418$); deseasonalizing is near zero
(0.086 [$-0.027$, 0.186]). The three non-replications are the two stronger-on-private effects (frequency shift,
smoothing) and level shift, whose sign flips (dev $-0.188$, private 0.109 [$-0.025$, 0.159]); under the registered rule,
dev $\rho$ inside the private interval, a stronger private effect also counts as a non-replication. In TimesFM,
per-window restoration concentrates just before the horizon and grows with depth (earlier four-model run,
\texttt{noise}: 0.068 at layer 0 to 0.412 at layer 16; exploratory, $n\approx300$; \fid{DE-01}). Depth \emph{locations} need two qualifications that every report renders: captured compute ranges from
13.9\% (Chronos-T5-Base) to 99.8\% (Timer) of a forecast (\fid{DE-05}), and Chronos-T5's last encoder block sits at 0.478
on the whole-stack axis (\fid{DE-04}). No selected layer was robust across three layer selectors for any model (\fid{DE-08}).

\paragraph{Answer to RQ4.} Depth-resolved reaction profiles are largely reproducible (6 of 9 corruptions); absolute depth
locations are comparable only after accounting for captured compute and the depth convention.

\subsection{Can looking inside predict forecast failure?}
\label{sec:practical}

If internal features are causal, do they tell a user when to distrust a forecast, or which model to use? For each model we
predict a series' log MASE (Spearman) and failure flag (AUROC) from free output-only features (quantile width, flatness,
catch22 of the context), then add the SAE-family activations at the last window (cross-fitted, series bootstrap, 941 dev
series, $n_{\text{boot}}=1000$; evidence class \emph{predictive}, since a feature that predicts error is not a cause of
it). The free baseline is strong (Spearman 0.76--0.85). For five of seven models (Chronos-2, TimesFM, Sundial,
Chronos-T5-Base, Chronos-Bolt) the gain's CI includes zero for both tasks (log-MASE Spearman gains $-0.004$ to $+0.005$,
AUROC $-0.010$ to $+0.008$). It is positive for the two weakest models, Timer (+0.030 [0.019, 0.043], baseline 0.787,
failure rate 0.30) and Time-MoE (+0.012 [0.005, 0.019], baseline 0.853, failure rate 0.62), robust across five fold seeds,
and no failure-AUROC gain excludes zero. Both gains were registered and confirm when the frozen procedure is refit on the
private split ($n=935$): Timer 0.0446 [0.0292, 0.0605], $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.002$; Time-MoE 0.011 [0.0032, 0.0194],
$p_{\text{Holm}}=0.012$ (14 dev comparisons were run and 2 selected, which is legitimate because the private split
referees). Routing each series to the model with the lowest predicted MASE does not help: baseline routing reaches mean MASE
1.948 [1.814, 2.070], baseline plus internals 1.964 [1.837, 2.093] (gap $-0.016$ [$-0.044$, 0.010], $p=0.258$), and both
are worse than always using Chronos-2 (1.856; $p=0.001$; oracle 1.523). Internals add information exactly where the free
baseline is weakest.

\subsection{What replicated on held-out data}
\label{sec:heldout}

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/heldout.pdf}
\caption{Claims re-tested on the sealed private split (971 series; panel a uses 800 of them). (a) Per-corruption

concept-transfer claims with private null 95th percentiles; (c) accuracy and peak CKA; crosses: dev, dots and bars:

One look at a fresh private corpus (v2 epoch 3: 971 series, no sample shared with any other split, seal verified)
tested a registry of 128 hypotheses, 82 of them replicable, each Holm family per claim type as registered
(Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}). \textbf{Confirmed:} the accuracy claim (Chronos-2 better on \texttt{mixture}, $\Delta$MASE 0.209
[0.119, 0.313], $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0002$, $n=290$; minimum detectable effect 0.133), the peak-CKA pair, 6 of 9
perturbation agreements, all 20 transfer claims, 10 of 31 testable single-feature causal claims, both structural claims
(no concept spans the panel: the private atlas has 10 concepts, at most 3 of 7 models each; most prominent features are
causally null) and both failure-prediction claims. \textbf{Not confirmed:} the cross-model causal claims, namely the
multi-model atlas concepts under the registered conjunctive rule (0 of 3: their private centroid cosines, 0.955--0.988,
beat the null's 95th percentile of 0.854--0.907 with $p\le0.007$, but the rule also required half of the member pairs to
reach cosine 0.9, and with three members only one pair did; the centroid component alone is a post hoc reading) and shared-input agreement (0 of 6 tested, 5 of 11 not testable; the best, Chronos-Bolt block 4
to Chronos-2 block 7 ``same'', reproduces its verdict with $p=0.011$ but misses Holm at 0.066). In all, 43 claims
confirm, 33 do not and 6 cannot be tested. Held out, models share what they select, the average effect of a multi-model concept
reproduces, and fine-grained concepts stay model-specific; series-by-series effect agreement across families is beyond what ablation can measure at these effect sizes
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). One procedural event, a crash before any statistic was written,
is disclosed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.
