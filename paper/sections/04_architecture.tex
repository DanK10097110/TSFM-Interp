\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\label{sec:layout}
The repository holds two separately installable packages: \texttt{tsfm\_benchmark} (one subpackage for
generation, audit and sealing, another for diversity and exchangeability) and \texttt{tsfm\_lens} (subpackages for models, extraction, analysis, SAEs and the report, plus the stage
orchestrator \texttt{pipeline.py}). The folder
\repodir{examples/panel7_v2} contains the report, configuration and every lightweight artifact of
the run analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:casestudy}, so the report and this paper's figures can be regenerated
without GPUs. The \texttt{main} branch is the clean publishable surface; a \texttt{dev} branch keeps the
research log, standalone study drivers and experiment configurations.

\subsection{Controlled benchmark generation}
\label{sec:benchmark}

Real data enter the benchmark only as leakage references, as realism calibration and as raw material for
explicitly labeled generators. The \emph{synthetic} tier touches no real data and has exact ground truth:
\texttt{parametric} is additive (trend, multiple seasonalities, AR noise, changepoints, anomalies, random
walk, intermittency, heteroskedasticity), and \texttt{random\_parametric} draws a recipe from one of eight
frozen archetypes (four more are opt-in). The \emph{real-derived} tier (\texttt{mixture},
\texttt{block\_bootstrap} \citep{kunsch1989block}, \texttt{sequential\_par}) inherits its source distribution
and has attributional ground truth. Because ``no leakage'' and ``realistic'' pull in opposite directions,
each sample carries its tier label and analyses report per tier.

Every sample passes a two-stage leakage gate against the Monash archive \citep{godahewa2021monash} (a cheap
prefilter, then banded DTW; \citealp{sakoe1978dtw}) and a cross-split near-duplicate check. Dev and private
splits use disjoint seed ranges mixed with SHA-256; every sample is hashed so that
\texttt{load\_sealed(verify=True)} detects tampering, and fresh private \emph{epochs} are minted by striding
the seed space. A separate validation package measures redundancy by shape matching and diversity in the
catch22 feature space \citep{lubba2019catch22} (participation-ratio dimension, \citealp{gao2017pr};
nearest-neighbor tails), never on a 2-D embedding, and tests dev/private exchangeability with an energy
distance \citep{szekely2013energy} plus TOST equivalence \citep{schuirmann1987tost} (v1 benchmark: energy
$p=0.51$, 21 of 22 features equivalent, \fid{MP-09}; the case study's v2 dev split against its private epoch: energy
$p=0.951$, 20 of 22 features equivalent, 6 near-duplicate pairs among 937{,}015).

Two perturbation families are built in: nine structured \emph{corruptions} (noise, detrend, deseasonalize,
frequency shift, level shift, spike, smooth, warp, dropout), which drive L3, and \emph{generator
counterfactuals}, which move one generative knob along a dose ladder with every random draw fixed. Dose~1.0
must reproduce the original bit-exactly, a free identity test that caught a real bug (\fid{BM-05}).

\subsection{Model adapters and capability tiers}
\label{sec:adapters}

A model enters through an adapter (\repo{tsfm_lens/tsfm_lens/models/base.py}) exposing \texttt{load},
\texttt{predict} (point forecast and quantiles in the original scale), the module, input preparation, a
forward pass and \texttt{token\_time\_spans}. The \emph{capability tier} is derived from which methods a
subclass overrides, never declared: 0 (black box), 1 (observable hidden states with spans), 2 (steerable:
single-pass context, so patching is possible) and 3 (decomposable attention). Stages above a model's tier
are skipped with the missing method named in the report. A missing capability
(\texttt{CapabilityUnavailable}) is a fact about the adapter; a measured failure of time localization
(\texttt{NotTimeLocalized}) is a fact about the model and routes it to behavioral analysis only.

New models use the zero-code \texttt{generic\_hf} adapter, which discovers layers and measures spans and
refuses rather than guesses (its span discovery reproduces all six hand-written adapters' spans exactly, mean
IoU 1.0, and recovers Timer's 96-step spans from measurement alone; \fid{MP-01}), or a copy of \repo{tsfm_lens/tsfm_lens/models/TEMPLATE_adapter.py} in \texttt{models/contrib/}.
\texttt{run.py -{}-check-adapter} then runs patch-identity and reach conformance, the alignment gate, span
discovery and capability checks. Table~\ref{tab:models} lists the models.

\subsection{Time alignment across architectures}
\label{sec:alignment}

Each token's span is measured from its impulse response (\texttt{extraction/span\_discovery.py}), gated on
peak-to-pedestal contrast and contiguity. Given spans $I_j$ and windows $W_w=[wL,(w+1)L)$ ($L=32$ by
default), tokens are overlap-pooled,
\begin{equation}
  P_{wj} = \frac{|I_j \cap W_w|}{\sum_{j'} |I_{j'} \cap W_w|}, \qquad \tilde h_w = \sum_j P_{wj}\, h_j ,
  \label{eq:pooling}
\end{equation}
and every cross-model analysis runs on $[\text{series},\text{window},\text{dim}]$ tensors
(Figure~\ref{fig:alignment}); an empty window raises rather than yielding a zero row. During extraction an
\emph{alignment gate} injects an impulse into each window and checks, per layer, that the matching pooled
window moves most, normalizing hits by their attainable ceiling (a 96-step token on 32-step windows reaches
at most one third; \fid{MP-02}), with the impulse amplitude self-calibrated per checkpoint and context
length.

\subsection{The stage pipeline and its gates}
\label{sec:stages}

An analysis is a pipeline of 19 stages (Figure~\ref{fig:stages}) writing typed artifacts. Stages skip
themselves when their artifacts exist; each declares the configuration keys it depends on, which are
fingerprinted so that a changed setting marks exactly the dependent artifacts stale. Three gates write their
decisions to JSON and the report names them as skip reasons: the \emph{tier} gate, the \emph{run-shape} gate
(comparison stages are dropped for one model) and the \emph{routing} gate. Extraction captures the residual
stream into a float16 zarr store, and every intervention goes through one primitive
(\texttt{hooks.token\_patch}), which raises if the model silently splits a batch.

\subsection{The report: designed to be readable by newcomers}
\label{sec:report}

The deliverable is one self-contained HTML file (\repo{examples/panel7_v2/report.html}), designed
with the intention that a reader without interpretability training can follow it, while an expert can audit
every number
(Figure~\ref{fig:report}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Question-first.} Seven parts pose plain questions (``Is the comparison fair?'', ``Where does
    the forecast form?'', ``What causally drives the forecast?'', \dots); each stage opens with a card stating
    its question, method, what good and bad results look like, and what it cannot tell you.
  \item \textbf{Every figure explained.} A caption plus a collapsible ``What does this mean?'' note (purpose,
    how to read, limitations): 530 notes in the example. A glossary and methods appendix are generated from
    the code.
  \item \textbf{Derived verdicts.} The scorecard is a pure reduction over artifacts with no model
    names hard-coded; each row shows the value, its reference (null, floor, ceiling or baseline) and the rule
    that yields the verdict.
  \item \textbf{Typed findings.} Each claim is a \texttt{Finding} record with evidence class, plain-language
    sentence and generated caveat: 240 in the example (89 descriptive, 54 behavioral, 49 causal, 42
    linear-translatable, 4 geometric, 2 illustrative).
  \item \textbf{Nothing silently missing.} Skipped stages render their gate reason, failed sections render
    visibly, and a multiplicity ledger (123 families, 4{,}238 comparisons) flags families that cannot reach
    $\alpha$.
\end{itemize}
The complete analysis is one command,
\begin{center}\texttt{python run.py -{}-config configs/examples/large\_4model.yaml}\end{center}
and smaller presets cover a CPU-only mock
smoke test, a single model and a pair.
