\section{Questions a Reader May Ask}
\label{app:faq}

Each answer gives the evidence, its source, and what remains unmeasured. Numbers are from the seven-model run unless an
identifier says otherwise.

\paragraph{Is the benchmark trustworthy?} Every sample passed a banded-DTW leakage gate (threshold 0.35) against Monash
reference series: 68 of 2{,}000 candidates were rejected, and the closest accepted sample sits at distance 0.352. The dev
split and the private epoch used for confirmation are exchangeable: energy-distance $p=0.951$, 20 of 22 catch22 features
equivalent by TOST (the other two inconclusive at this $n$), tier and family composition indistinguishable ($p=0.94$ and
$p=0.9999$), and 6 near-duplicate pairs among 937{,}015 cross-split pairs. Diversity is measured in catch22 space: the dev
split has effective dimension 5.37 of 22 features (private 5.57) and a near-collision fraction of 0.052. Each sample is
labeled with its tier, so every result can be split by tier. The realism report compares raw moments and is not
informative across scales; its only usable statistic, spectral centroid, differs from the reference by 0.022.

\paragraph{Why a synthetic benchmark rather than raw real data?} Four reasons, each measured. Ground truth: only
generated series record their components, so only they can validate a probe (P1). Representation: on real Monash rows
TimesFM's representations have lower effective dimension than on the synthetic corpus (1.76 against 2.66) and more dead
dictionary atoms (\fid{MN-20}). Measurability: weakly predictable real-derived series receive flat forecasts in 67--100\%
of cases (\fid{BM-06}); across ablation panels, noise-like contexts are flat 82.3\% of the time against 8.5\% for smooth
ones, and a flat forecast gives an ablation nothing to move. Contamination: public archives may be in the models'
pretraining data. Real data are still used, each for a stated purpose: real-derived series (410 of 965) for realism and
per-tier checks, about 4{,}000 raw Monash windows per target only as extra dictionary-training rows, and the Monash
archive only as the leakage reference.

\paragraph{Is there enough data?} It depends on the claim, and each claim's power is stated. Not power-limited: the 20
transfer claims all reach the Holm floor (AUC 0.919--0.970 against null 95th percentiles of 0.70--0.75); the accuracy
claim's minimum detectable $\Delta$MASE is 0.133 against an observed 0.209; the CKA interval is $[0.401, 0.427]$ against a
null of 0.038. Power-limited: single-feature causal claims (each feature is tested on its 8 strongest-firing series, and
19 of the 21 that did not confirm never reached the dev $p$ floor), per-target concepts (178 of 672 candidates never fire
on any series, so more rows do not help; \fid{SH-20}), and the shared-input agreement rung, which more shared series do
not rescue (\fid{MN-30}).

\paragraph{Do the models forecast sensibly on this corpus?} Five of seven beat the seasonal naive forecast on 77--85\% of
series (Chronos-2 0.845, TimesFM 0.823, Sundial 0.798, Chronos-Bolt 0.784, Chronos-T5-Base 0.765); Timer does on 0.695 and
Time-MoE on 0.378 (Table~\ref{tab:character}). The models are therefore analyzed while doing the task they were trained
for, except Time-MoE, whose results the paper reads with its accuracy in mind.

\paragraph{Why define concepts by causal effect rather than by activation or correlation?} Because correlation misleads
here, measurably. 83.5\% of the most prominent features have no causal effect on held-out data (\fid{MN-14}); attention
mass on seasonal lags is uncorrelated with the seasonal power lost when the head is ablated (\fid{CA-04}); two sparse
features can correlate at $\rho=0.80$ with no shared top series (\fid{MN-19}); features aligned with a ground-truth field do
not mediate that field's effect (\fid{MN-17}); and on a planted forecaster a decoy concept correlates with forecast error
in five of five seeds while its causal effect is null (\fid{MN-31}). An activation-based concept can be real and still
irrelevant to the forecast; the causal definition asks the question that matters for forecasting.

\paragraph{Is zeroing a feature a fair intervention?} For a TopK dictionary, zero is a feature's resting value: at most 32
of 192--6{,}144 atoms are nonzero on any token, so the ablated state lies inside the dictionary's normal range, unlike
zeroing a dense neuron. The comparison is between two passes through the same reconstruction, so the dictionary's error
is in both arms. The null removes exactly the feature's per-token amount along another direction, on the same series,
with the same seed for sampled models, after a reach probe has shown that patching this layer can move the forecast. Two
alternatives were not run: mean ablation, and ablating against the clean activation; the latter differs only through the
model's nonlinear response to the reconstruction error.

\paragraph{How do you know a causal feature is not chance?} Four checks. (i) Calibration on a known answer: on the planted
forecaster's control layer, which has no planted effect, the per-cell false-positive rate is at most 0.003 while planted
effects are found with sensitivity 0.70--1.00 (\fid{MN-29}). (ii) Counting against an empirical chance rate, estimated
per cell by scoring each null draw against the others (median 0.018): 442 feature-channel cells clear against 178.75
expected (2.47 times chance), and 22 of 55 targets are individually significant (BH). At the level of whole features the
excess is smaller: 194 of 1{,}022 scorable candidates clear at least one channel against at most 144.95 expected if
channels were independent. (iii) Held-out replication: 10 registered single-feature claims confirm on new series at the
attainable $p$ floor after adaptive redraws to 1{,}000 null draws. (iv) Null strictness: a stricter null that draws the
random direction from the layer's own activation covariance also passes the planted test (sensitivity 0.916), but on the
earlier four-model run it leaves only 1.13 times chance. Causal effects therefore exceed random directions clearly and
typical on-manifold directions only slightly; the planned confirmatory run registers its claims against the stricter
null.

\paragraph{How large are the effects?} Small relative to the forecast and to the dictionary's own error. On the earlier
four-model run the median single-feature effect was 0.0395 context standard deviations, 3.5 times below the change from
substituting the reconstruction (\fid{MN-15}); among the cells that clear in the seven-model run, the median effect is
1.24 times the null's 95th percentile. The paper therefore makes no claim that single features explain forecasts; it
claims that specific, replicable directions exist and characterizes what they do.

\paragraph{How robust are the sparse dictionaries?} Each target has three replicate dictionaries, and 9 of 11 atlas
concepts are stable across them. Held-out fraction of variance explained has medians of 0.647 (TimesFM) to 0.878
(Chronos-T5-Base) across models, and only 5 of 56 targets pass the strict admission gate (held-out fidelity $\ge0.85$,
forecast change under substitution $\le0.25$ MASE, dead-rate bound); results restricted to admitted targets are part of
the planned confirmatory run. Joint crosscoders were tested as the alternative and lost to independent dictionaries
matched afterwards (0.2787 against 0.3204, $p=0.002$; \fid{SH-10}), which is why \tool{} uses one TopK dictionary per
layer.

\paragraph{How are token time frames aligned across models?} In three steps, all measured rather than declared. (1) Each
token's span is measured by injecting an impulse at every time step and recording which token responds most, gated on
peak-to-pedestal contrast ($\ge4$) and contiguity ($\ge0.95$); measured spans equal the hand-written adapters' on six
checkpoints (IoU 1.0) and recover Timer's 96-step tokens (\fid{MP-01}). (2) Tokens are overlap-pooled onto common 32-step
windows (Equation~\ref{eq:pooling}): Chronos-T5's one-step tokens are averaged 32 to a window, TimesFM's 32-step tokens
map one to one, and each Timer token covers three windows, which then share one value (the alignment gate's ceiling
for Timer is therefore one third). The context of 480 steps is a multiple of 96 so that Timer's tokens tile it exactly
(at 512 its span discovery refuses). (3) An alignment gate injects an impulse into each window and checks per layer that
the matching window moves most; all models pass at their shallowest layer, and the gate's per-layer record shows that
deep layers of Sundial, Time-MoE and Timer lose time localization (diagonal hit 0.07--0.27 against chance 0.067), which
the report renders and which qualifies window-level claims there. Values are normalized by each model's own input
scaling, verified by a scale-equivariance test (residuals $2.7\times10^{-7}$ to 0.037 context standard deviations);
effects are compared across models in units of each feature's own null 95th percentile; depth is the fraction of the
whole stack. Window sizes other than 32 have not yet been compared.

\paragraph{Do sampled models make the causal comparison noisy?} Every compared pair of calls to Sundial or Chronos-T5 uses
the same seed. Unseeded, back-to-back Sundial calls differ by up to 0.50, and a null seeded differently from its baseline
once made Sundial look causally inert (\fid{PM-09}); repeat-run noise floors are reported per model (Sundial 0.14,
Chronos-T5-Base 0.24 mean $|\Delta$MASE$|$, the deterministic models 0).

\paragraph{Could the private split have influenced the claims?} The registry (128 hypotheses, sha256 recorded) was
frozen before the private epoch was generated; the epoch shares no sample hash with any of seven earlier splits (one
candidate epoch was discarded unopened for overlapping an old split); \texttt{confirm} ran once, and a crash before any
statistic was written is disclosed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. Registered claims are cuts from larger dev pools (transfer
20 of 1{,}124 candidates, causal 32 of 113, agreement 11 of 25, atlas 4 of the 9 stable concepts), which is legitimate
because the private split referees them, and is why only registered claims are called confirmed.

\paragraph{How are multiple comparisons handled?} Dev results are exploratory and labeled so. The ledger groups 4{,}238
tests into 123 named families with Holm or BH and flags families that cannot reach significance; the battery's 8{,}705
cells are judged by their count against the empirical chance rate. Held-out claims use Holm per registered claim type.

\paragraph{Why CKA, given its known problems?} It is used only against a shuffled-series null at the same layer pair with
a series-level bootstrap, reported as one of seven pair metrics that are never pooled, and never read as lineage, since an
untrained pair can score higher than a trained one (\fid{MN-01}). The reported peak is a maximum over layer pairs; its
held-out replication (0.411) is measured at the pair selected on dev.

\paragraph{Were settings chosen after seeing results?} Battery settings ($k=8$ rows, 16 null draws) were fixed on the
four-model run before the seven-model concept stage ran, after larger settings did not recover power (\fid{SH-20}); at the
atlas threshold of 0.9 the real concept count was about twice its shuffle-null mean on that earlier run (19 against
9.62); family silhouettes
are flat (0.25--0.32) across cuts from 6 to 16, which is why families are reported as a reading vocabulary.

\paragraph{Does this generalize to other models?} The pipeline is model-agnostic behind the adapter interface, and two of
the seven models load through the zero-code path. Of eight checkpoints probed, five crashed in their configuration loader
and need hand-written adapters (\fid{MN-25}); models whose tokens read disjoint lags (Lag-Llama) receive behavioral
analysis only.

\section{Statistical Details}
\label{app:stats}

\paragraph{Paired series bootstrap.} With per-series differences $d_s=m_{a,s}-m_{b,s}$ and $B=n_{\text{boot}}$
resampled means $\bar d^*_b$, the interval is the percentile interval and
$p=\min\!\big(1,\max(\tfrac1B,\,2\min\{\tfrac1B\#(\bar d^*_b\le 0),\tfrac1B\#(\bar d^*_b\ge 0)\})\big)$
(\texttt{analysis/stats.py}; floor per \citealp{phipson2010permutation}). Window-level statistics (CKA,
fingerprints) resample series with all their windows \citep{cameron2008cluster}.

\paragraph{Multiplicity.} Holm: $\tilde p_{(i)}=\max_{j\le i}\min(1,(m-j+1)p_{(j)})$ \citep{holm1979}; with
$p\ge 1/B$ the smallest attainable value is $m/B$, which the ledger reports per family, flagging $m/B>\alpha$.
BH: $\tilde q_{(i)}=\min_{j\ge i}\min(1,\tfrac{m}{j}p_{(j)})$ \citep{benjamini1995fdr}.

\paragraph{Profile-matched ablation null.} For feature $i$ with rows $R_i$ and $J=16$ unit directions $u_j$, the null
removes at every token $t$ the amount $|z_i(t)|\,\|w_i\|\,u_j$ (the legacy null removed one batch-averaged amount
$\bar a\,W_d u_j$); channel $c$ clears if
$\frac{1}{|R_i|}\sum_{r\in R_i}|\Delta^{(c)}_{i,r}| > q_{0.95}\{|\Delta^{(c)}_{u_j,r}|: j\le J, r\in R_i\}$. A null
with no spread is reported as degenerate and not scored.

\paragraph{Transfer.} The statistic is $\max_f \mathrm{AUC}(a_f;S)$ over destination features; its null
recomputes the same maximum on stratum-matched random $k$-sets, so the feature search is inside the null. The
reverse leg scores the chosen feature's own top-$k$ by the source activation. $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\ge
\text{obs}\})/(1+n)$; a registered claim uses $\max(p,p_{\text{rev}})$ (intersection--union), Holm across claims.

\paragraph{Shared-input agreement.} On $U$, statistic (i) is the Spearman concordance of the two sides' signed
level effects across series, and (ii) the cosine of their level-removed shape vectors, each channel in its own
null units. Each side's floor ablates random feature sets matched to its activation on $U$ and re-scores against
the other side's measured effect; agreement exceeds both floors' $q_{0.95}$, ``acts differently'' falls below
both $q_{0.05}$.

\paragraph{Minimum detectable effect.} Before the private result is read, recentred per-series differences are
shifted by a candidate effect and re-tested with the same bootstrap; bisection finds the smallest shift detected
with power 0.8 (\texttt{analysis/power.py}).

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

\repodir{examples/panel7_v2} holds the configuration, the report and every stage's JSON/parquet
artifacts plus the confirmation (activations and SAE weights omitted for size); \texttt{paper/figures/make\_figures.py -{}-run DIR}
regenerates all data figures. From \texttt{tsfm\_lens/}, \texttt{python run.py -{}-config
configs/panel7\_v2.yaml} reproduces the run (add \texttt{-{}-stages report} to re-render, and
\texttt{-{}-verify-provenance runs/panel7\_v2\_dev} to diff environments). The private split (v2 epoch 3, 971 series) was
opened once (2026-10-02), so re-confirming requires a fresh epoch. Two events are disclosed. A first epoch minted for
this look (v2 epoch 1) was rejected before any model saw it, because 716 of its 961 series equalled series of a split an
earlier confirmation had consumed (epoch seeds depend only on the seed and epoch number). And the first confirm attempt crashed after every private battery
finished, while assembling the claim ledger (a fixed family map lacked the failure-prediction family), before any
statistic was written: \texttt{confirmation.json} did not exist, no private number was read, the registry hash and every
frozen specification were unchanged, and the restart differed only by the crash fix. It is one test of the registry run
twice for compute reasons; the artifact records \texttt{repeated\_look: false}. Separately, 19 of the 21 non-confirmed single-feature claims
carry a stored reading of ``untestable'' in \texttt{confirmation.json}; they were tested and not detected, the report
recomputes the reading at render time, and no verdict is affected. Environment, as recorded in the run's
\texttt{run\_manifest.json}: Python 3.12.13, numpy 2.1.0, PyTorch 2.12.0 (CUDA 12.9 build), transformers 4.57.6,
timesfm 2.0.2, chronos-forecasting 2.3.1, zarr 2.18.7 (zarr~3 stores read back empty under the v2 API), pandas 2.3.3,
scikit-learn 1.7.2; load-bearing pins and their reasons are in \repo{DEPENDENCIES.md}. Tests: 2{,}520 (\texttt{tsfm\_lens}) and 125 (\texttt{tsfm\_benchmark}), run in continuous integration.
Recorded stage wall-clock time on one RTX
A5000: 75{,}900 s in total, of which \texttt{sae} 14{,}614 s and \texttt{concepts} 55{,}608 s (shared-input
agreement 45{,}189 s); every other stage under 33 minutes.
