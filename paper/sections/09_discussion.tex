\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Shared inputs, model-specific mechanisms.} The most robust cross-model result is about inputs: models with
different tokenizations, depths and training objectives single out the same series for a concept, on two corpora and on
raw real windows. Whole-layer geometry is shared as well: all 21 model pairs exceed shuffled-series nulls on both
corpora and on a fresh sealed split, most strongly at early, input-encoding layers, once windows are compared without
averaging away their internal order. Yet at the grain of individual causal effects,
concepts are organized by model, and agreement on the same series is detected only between two closely related Chronos
models. One consistent reading is that the panel converges on \emph{what} in a series matters (its level, its
seasonality) while implementing the response differently. This is a weaker form of convergence than the representational
convergence argued for in vision and language \citep{li2016convergent,huh2024platonic}, and our instruments locate the
boundary: input agreement is measurable and replicates, whereas effect agreement across architecture families is below
what ablation can resolve at these effect sizes (Section~\ref{sec:knownanswer}).

\paragraph{What the causal evidence supports.} Single SAE features clearly beat isotropic random directions of the same
size but barely beat directions drawn from the activation distribution. More dictionary data, a more powerful test and
gradient-selected candidates do not change this (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). We read this as a property of the models at
this level of description: the forecast-relevant information in a layer is spread over many directions, and removing
any one typical direction moves the forecast by a comparable amount. Claims that a single feature ``is'' a forecasting
concept should therefore be stated relative to an on-manifold null, since against an off-manifold null a typical direction
already looks special; this is the time-series counterpart of the illusions documented for subspace interventions
\citep{makelov2024illusion}.

\paragraph{Implications for method.} Three lessons generalize beyond this study. First, activation-based selection is
not a substitute for intervention: most prominent features have no causal effect, and attention mass does not predict
head importance. Second, nulls must be validated on a known answer before they are trusted; the plausible uniform null
inflated the count of causal features 2.7-fold (293 against 110) and failed the planted test on every seed. Third, a negative verdict needs a
test of sensitivity: our agreement rung never produces a false ``same'', but it also rarely detects a planted shared
concept, so its silence carries no information. Exploratory pipelines with thousands of comparisons \citep{gelman2014garden}
benefit from the same register-then-confirm discipline used here, which in our study confirmed between half and two thirds
of the registered claims (43 of 82 and 41 of 63).

\paragraph{Practical use.} The internal features add information about forecast failure only for the two least
accurate models, whose forecasts fail most often, and do not improve model selection. For practitioners, the more
immediate value of the system is diagnostic: behavioral checks such as calibration and missing-value handling, and the
documented failures of instruments that would otherwise produce confident, wrong results.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Models.} The study covers seven transformer models with contiguous tokens; models whose tokens read
disjoint lags, such as Lag-Llama, receive reduced scope. With seven models, contrasts between models are confounded with
architecture, size and training data. Timer's features do not exceed chance, so its results carry no evidence about causal
concepts. The analysis is univariate: Chronos-2's cross-series group attention is not measured (\fid{PM-13}), and
Chronos-T5's decoder and Sundial's flow head are not captured.

\paragraph{Time alignment.} A common time grid is no finer than the coarsest token: no window rule recovers
resolution finer than Timer's 96-step token, and peak layer locations depend on the window size (\fid{SH-23}).
Concatenated windows, which the similarity results use, are computed on 512 series per corpus, the size at which the
Gram matrices fit in memory. SAE comparisons do not depend on the window rule. Dictionaries are trained on window averages, which is conservative
for 1-step models: trained on its own tokens, Chronos-T5-Base's dictionary explains token variance far better and yields
more causal features, while the top series it shares with TimesFM's dictionary change within seed noise (\fid{MP-11}).

\paragraph{Ground truth and external validity.} Ground truth is exact only for the synthetic tier and encodes the
generators' assumptions; 12 of 33 concept parts are dominated by one real-derived generator, and overlap with the models'
pretraining data cannot be verified. Real data are part of the analysis: 410 of the 965 v2 dev series are real-derived,
and the dictionaries are trained with about 4{,}000 additional real Monash windows per target. Raw real benchmarks are
nonetheless a weaker basis for the causal questions. Real rows give TimesFM representations of lower effective dimension
than the synthetic corpus (1.76 against 2.66) and more dead dictionary atoms (\fid{MN-20}); weakly predictable
real-derived series receive flat forecasts in 67--100\% of cases, leaving an ablation nothing to move (\fid{BM-06}); and
public archives may be in the models' pretraining data. Raw real data therefore enter as an external check rather than
as a discovery corpus: concepts are discovered where ground truth exists, and the v3 transfer claims are then confirmed
on 800 raw real windows held out from every dev stage (20 of 20).

\paragraph{Scope of the confirmation.} Held-out confirmation covers accuracy, geometry, perturbation agreement, transfer,
single-feature causal effects, atlas structure and failure prediction, on two corpora with one private split each, and the registered window-geometry claims were tested on a second sealed
v3 split. The
20 confirmed v2 transfers derive from two source concepts and the v3 transfers from three source models, so the transfer
result rests on a small number of concepts. Registered claims are selected from larger dev pools; this is legitimate
because the private split referees them, but it means that unregistered dev results remain exploratory.

\paragraph{Effect sharing on the same series.} Per-series agreement of causal effects across architectures is not
measurable by ablation at the effect sizes these models have. Both the shared-input rung and its subspace redesign fail
the sensitivity requirement of the known-answer test (Section~\ref{sec:knownanswer}), and \tool{} therefore compares
effects across models at the level of concept profiles.

\paragraph{Dictionaries, interventions and usability.} Concept results depend on dictionary size, sparsity, revival and
seed; the system measures this dependence but cannot remove it, and feature death tracks layer geometry (alive atoms
scale with effective dimension with a log-log exponent of 1.02, $\rho=0.58$ over 45 cells; \fid{MP-05}). Mean ablation
confirms 69 of 91 v3 causal features but only 13 of 29 dense ones, so for dense features the verdict depends on the
intervention; resample ablation and interventions on the clean activation were not run. The controls
catch only failures for which a known-answer case exists. Readability for newcomers is a design goal; no user study has
measured comprehension.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Interpreting TSFMs is harder than it looks: tokens mean different things in different models, real series have no
annotated ground truth, and causal effects are small enough that measurement artifacts dominate unless they are
controlled. \tool{} addresses these problems with a controlled benchmark, measured time alignment, null- and reach-gated
causal concepts validated on a planted forecaster, and a register-then-confirm protocol, behind one command and a report
written for newcomers. Across seven TSFMs and two corpora, different architectures share above-null representational geometry, a
similarity that the common window average had hidden, and select the same inputs for a concept, while their causal
machinery is organized by model; causal features act mainly on level and seasonality but are only
weakly specific against on-manifold directions; activation prominence does not reveal causal importance; and
depth-resolved reaction profiles replicate for most corruptions on the primary corpus and about half on the second. Each conclusion is stated with its evidence class, and each held-out
claim was registered before the private data were opened.
