\section{Known-Answer Validation of the Causal Instruments}
\label{sec:knownanswer}

Every causal count in Section~\ref{sec:casestudy} rests on a null, and a null can be wrong without raising any error.
Each instrument is therefore first tested on a forecaster whose answer is known.

\paragraph{The planted forecaster.} \tool{} ships a mock forecaster, \texttt{mock\_planted}. One block writes known unit
directions into the residual stream on the series that have a given property (thresholded readouts of the patch mean,
slope, residual deviation, seasonal projections and a spike statistic, each firing on 10--30\% of the corpus), and the
forecast head applies a known effect on level, trend, seasonality or dispersion. Decoys look like concepts:
\emph{input-only} directions with zero causal effect and \emph{sub-null} directions with a weak effect. A control layer
after the planted block has no planted effect. For cross-model tests, a pair of architectures (widths 64 and 96, depths
5 and 7, rotated residual bases) shares planted concepts, plus a twin with the opposite effect.

\paragraph{Protocol.} A scorer reads only the planted manifest and the run's artifacts. Pass criteria were fixed before
any data were seen (Table~\ref{tab:knownanswer}): a per-cell false-positive rate of at most 0.10 on the control layer and
a sensitivity of at least 0.5 for nulls, and a recall of at least 0.8 for candidate ranking. Each is evaluated on five
construction seeds at one effect size; seed 0 was used to tune the attribution rule and seeds 1--4 were held out.

\paragraph{Results.} Both nulls used in the study pass on every seed (Table~\ref{tab:knownanswer}). The test
discriminates: a null that removes one uniform, batch-averaged amount instead of the feature's own per-token profile
fails on every seed, because a strong or dense atom clears it on size alone, and on a four-model development run it
inflates the count of causal features from 110 to 293 (\fid{MN-29}). On seed 0 the profile-matched null keeps every
sub-null decoy from clearing, and raises the adjusted Rand index of the atlas against the planted concepts from 0.336 to
0.469 and sharing-class accuracy from 0.364 to 0.714.

The agreement rung R5 is specific but insensitive. It never calls a decoy ``same'', so its positive verdicts can be
trusted, but it rarely detects a concept that two planted architectures truly share, and quadrupling the number of shared
series does not change this (\fid{MN-30}). An absent ``same'' verdict on real models is therefore not evidence of a
difference. Known-answer tests establish what an instrument can detect and how often it is fooled in a world whose
answer is known; they do not establish that its verdicts on real models are correct.
