\section{Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:statistics}

Four mechanisms control how often a report with thousands of comparisons is fooled (Table~\ref{tab:claims}).

\paragraph{The series is the resampling unit.} Windows of a series share its trend, noise and scale, so every
interval and $p$-value resamples whole series \citep{efron1993bootstrap,cameron2008cluster}: paired bootstrap
for accuracy, cluster bootstrap for CKA, validation-series intervals for stitching, paired cluster bootstrap for
patching. Splits are by series and stratified, never the first rows of a corpus that is stored grouped by task.

\paragraph{A multiplicity ledger.} Every test belongs to a named family, corrected by Holm for confirmatory families and
by Benjamini--Hochberg for discovery-oriented atlas transfers. Resampled $p$-values are floored at $1/n_{\text{boot}}$
\citep{phipson2010permutation}, so a Holm family of $m$ tests cannot go below $m/n_{\text{boot}}$. The ledger computes
this minimum for each family and flags families that can never reach significance, a problem that grows quadratically
with the number of models compared. The seven-model run has 123 families with 4{,}238 comparisons; its strictest
accuracy family (105 tests over 21 model pairs) has a minimum Holm $p$ of 0.021 at $n_{\text{boot}}=5000$. Two kinds of
test are judged outside the ledger: the 8{,}705 feature-channel cells of the ablation battery are judged by their count
against the empirical chance rate, and shared-input agreement tests by their own per-test floors.

\paragraph{References measured in the run itself.} Every effect is read against a reference measured in the same run:
repeat-run noise floors, shuffled-series nulls, input-feature baselines, random-direction nulls, activation-matched
floors or replicate-seed ceilings. Before the private split is opened, \texttt{confirm} also simulates each claim's
minimum detectable effect, for example a $\Delta$MASE of 0.133 at $n=290$ with 80\% power, so that a non-replication can be
read as either an absent effect or an underpowered test.

\paragraph{Register, then confirm once.} The \texttt{register} stage freezes hypotheses from dev results together with
the content hash of each source artifact, and hashes the registry. \texttt{confirm} refuses mismatched hashes, requires
the private seal to verify, tests each claim once and refuses to overwrite its own artifact; a forced rerun is recorded as
a \texttt{repeated\_look}. A consumed split is replaced by a fresh epoch, which is checked for sample-hash overlap with
every earlier split of every corpus version. This is pre-registration \citep{nosek2018preregistration} implemented in
software, and it guards against adaptive reuse of the holdout \citep{dwork2015reusable,gelman2014garden}. Findings that
were not registered cannot be confirmed after the fact.
