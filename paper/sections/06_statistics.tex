\section{Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:statistics}

Four mechanisms control how often a report with thousands of comparisons is fooled (Table~\ref{tab:claims}).

\paragraph{The series is the resampling unit.} Windows of a series share its trend, noise and scale, so every
interval and $p$-value resamples whole series \citep{efron1993bootstrap,cameron2008cluster}: paired bootstrap
for accuracy, cluster bootstrap for CKA, validation-series intervals for stitching, paired cluster bootstrap for
patching. Splits are by series and stratified, never head slices of a task-grouped corpus.

\paragraph{A multiplicity ledger.} Every test belongs to a named family (Holm for confirmatory families, BH for
discovery-oriented atlas transfers). Resampled $p$-values are floored at $1/n_{\text{boot}}$
\citep{phipson2010permutation}, so a Holm family of $m$ tests cannot go below $m/n_{\text{boot}}$; the ledger
computes this minimum per family and flags families that can never be significant, a failure that grows
quadratically with the number of models. The example has 123 families and 4{,}238 comparisons; its strictest L0
family (105 tests over 21 pairs) has a minimum Holm $p$ of 0.021 at $n_{\text{boot}}=5000$.

\paragraph{References from the run itself.} Effects are read against repeat-run noise floors, shuffled-series
nulls, input-feature baselines, random-direction nulls, activation-matched floors and replicate-seed ceilings.
Before the private split is opened, \texttt{confirm} simulates each claim's minimum detectable effect (e.g.\
$\Delta$MASE 0.133 at $n=290$, 80\% power), so a non-replication reads as ``absent'' or ``underpowered''.

\paragraph{Register, then confirm once.} \texttt{register} freezes hypotheses from dev results together with the
content hash of each source artifact, and hashes the registry. \texttt{confirm} refuses mismatched hashes,
requires the private seal to verify, tests each claim once and refuses to overwrite its artifact; a forced rerun
is recorded as \texttt{repeated\_look}, and a consumed split is replaced by a fresh epoch, checked for sample-hash overlap
with every earlier split of every corpus version (an epoch minted for the case study was rejected unopened because 716 of
its 961 series regenerated a split an earlier confirmation had consumed). This is
pre-registration \citep{nosek2018preregistration} in software, guarding against adaptive holdout reuse
\citep{dwork2015reusable}; unregistered findings cannot be rescued.
