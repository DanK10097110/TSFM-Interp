# How much agreement is visible at the output?

A source model's response to an edited history predicts part of a second model's response on the same history. The result depends on which part of the forecast is compared. A prospective follow-up on two new datasets improved prediction of the complete response by **5.1–8.7%** across both transfer directions when it borrowed the source model's temporal shape and kept the forecast level with the destination's classical predictor. Matching the correct series accounts for **0.46–5.08 percentage points** of that improvement in a later donor check.

The predictor observes the source model's output on both versions of each test history. It learns from destination outputs on 128 calibration series. Its target is the full change in the destination model's median forecast under an **input** edit. The hidden-transport experiment has a different target, the change under a localized **internal** intervention. These experiments provide complementary evidence and their recovery scores have different denominators. [Hidden transport protocol](../transport/PROTOCOL.md)

## Fresh evidence for transferring shape

For each edited series, decompose the 16-lead response into its mean and its deviation around that mean. Fit a target predictor for each component using ten clean-history summaries plus the responses of two classical forecasters. These forecasters fit a 16-lag ridge autoregression or search for similar histories inside that series' 128 observations. A second model adds the other TSFM's response when predicting the **centered** component. The destination's classical estimate of the mean is retained. Use the same 128 M4 daily calibration series, four fixed input stimuli, and dose for every test domain.

| Fresh test | Source → destination | Shape-only gain, 95% interval | Simultaneous lower bound | Full-vector single-coefficient gain | Correct-item advantage over shuffled source |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Shenzhen traffic, 48 series | Bolt → TimesFM | **6.54%** [5.61, 7.38] | 5.47% | 5.38% | 0.46 pp |
| Shenzhen traffic, 48 series | TimesFM → Bolt | **8.23%** [5.65, 11.54] | 5.32% | −31.16% | 1.35 pp |
| Bitbrains random, 160 VMs | Bolt → TimesFM | **5.05%** [4.64, 5.43] | 4.59% | 8.35% | 1.15 pp |
| Bitbrains random, 160 VMs | TimesFM → Bolt | **8.74%** [6.96, 10.61] | 6.76% | −21.43% | 5.08 pp |

Gain is the relative decrease in squared error of the **full, signed, 16-lead response**, relative to the same split-component classical predictor. First compute the ratio separately for each waveform, then average the four ratios. All intervals condition on the fitted calibration models. Each bootstrap draw resamples whole series with replacement and carries both forecasts, signs, leads and all four stimuli together. The ordinary 95% interval uses the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles of 4,000 draws. The simultaneous lower bound uses the 1.25th percentile, corresponding to a one-sided .05/4 Bonferroni rule. All four registered bounds exceed zero. In the exploratory source-donor check, one donor assignment transfers the source residual for all four stimuli, shuffling within quartiles of test-history roughness while holding all classical predictions and weights fixed. Wrong donors retained 3.66–6.88% gain, and matching the correct source added the amounts shown (1,999 shuffles, raw one-sided p=.0005–.0015). Source-specific information accounts for a fraction of the full algorithm's gain. ## Why the controls changed the conclusion

- **Persistence can generate nearly perfect agreement.** On 192 M4 daily held-out items, the median cosine between the models' full input responses was .997 for a ramp and .997 for a recent step. A zero-parameter predictor repeating the change in the final observation at all 16 leads recovered 95.9% and 97.4% of Bolt's corresponding response energy, and 92.7% and 92.6% of TimesFM's. An interior pulse preserving the last observation had cross-model median cosine .368. For a positive control, both models translated all 16 predicted values by .35 under a global .35 input shift, with worst absolute error below .000003. [Diagnostics](results.json)
- **Pair matching alone can pass when the transferred calibration hurts.** Relative to a ridge on ten clean-history features, the first source-augmentation rule gained 4.1% [0.9, 7.5] and 0.2% [−6.5, 5.6] on M4 daily, then 11.7% [5.6, 17.5] and 10.6% [−0.2, 20.8] on 96 M4 weekly items (Bolt → TimesFM first). On 160 Seattle sensors evaluated at one common calendar window it lost 9.2% and 26.5%. Correct series pairing beat the roughness-matched donor null in all six cases, adjusted p=.003. The null tests whether matching the item helps, while the absolute gain tests how well the entire calibrated predictor works. [Protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Outcomes and intervals](results.json)
- **Level and shape can move independently.** On 160 unused Bitbrains fast-storage machines, two classical forecasters beat the clean-history baseline by 32.7% for predicting TimesFM responses and 9.9% for Bolt. Adding the other TSFM on top improved full-response recovery by 9.0% [7.3, 10.6] in the first direction but worsened it by 29.5% [−43.2, −16.9] in reverse. The reverse shape-only subproblem improved by 28.6% [23.8, 32.5], while its mean-level subproblem worsened by 62.3% [−85.8, −41.7]. This supplied the motivation for the prospective shape-only test above. [Classical protocol](CLASSICAL_PROTOCOL.md) · [Classical comparison](classical_results.json)
- **A single calendar window overstated the Seattle failure.** A fresh grid paired 48 new sensors with 16 new dates, disjoint from 80 calibration sensors on eight earlier dates. The frozen daily rule gained 2.8% [−5.1, 11.2] and 2.4% [−7.8, 10.6] against the respective daily clean predictors, so the original large negative did **not** replicate over dates. Local refitting yielded 6.5% [−0.3, 11.8] and 7.3% [3.4, 11.5] against local clean predictors, missing the registered bidirectional criterion. The two-way intervals resample sensors and calendar dates separately. Correctly matching both sensor and date improved on shuffles along either axis in both directions (p=.001). [Grid protocol](SEATTLE_PROTOCOL.md) · [Grid results](seattle_results.json)

## Scope and reproduction

These are response-prediction studies on two frozen forecasters and four prespecified edited waveforms. They establish a behavioral baseline for this calibration budget and this projection of the outputs. The model-specificity of causally transported hidden features requires internal interventions. Outside the Seattle grid, intervals over series describe one common sampling window per source and treat IDs as units. Shared road patterns, workloads, multi-series dependence, model pretraining and uncertainty in the M4 training sample can change the appropriate uncertainty for other populations.

The SHA-256 source hashes, series IDs and standardized-history hashes are retained in [primary items](items.json), [Bitbrains fast-storage items](bitbrains_items.json), [Seattle grid](seattle_grid_items.json) and [shape-study items](shape_items.json). These ID selections exclude the historical 800-window external benchmark; the primary study also excludes the localized transport experiment's first 640 items and the previous 24-item steering check. No private benchmark was consumed. Distinct IDs establish sample separation for these analyses; they cannot certify novelty relative to pretraining.

From the repository root, the compact arrays support reanalysis with NumPy, SciPy and PyArrow. The first two commands regenerate the selection manifest and original responses if rerunning inference is desired. Set `PYTHONPATH=.:tsfm_lens`, `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=2`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS=2` and `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` for the recorded CPU setting.

```bash
python research/activation_transfer/transport/prepare.py --cache /tmp/tsfm_activation_transport_v1
python research/activation_transfer/controls/collect.py --transport-manifest /tmp/tsfm_activation_transport_v1/manifest.json
python research/activation_transfer/controls/analyze.py
python research/activation_transfer/controls/classical.py --analyze-only
python research/activation_transfer/controls/seattle.py --analyze-only
python research/activation_transfer/controls/shape.py --analyze-only
python -m pytest -q research/activation_transfer/controls/test_controls.py
```

To repeat the new forecasts, replace `--analyze-only` in `classical.py` and `shape.py` with the same `--transport-manifest` argument and omit it in `seattle.py`. Collection additionally uses the pinned GIFT-Eval snapshot, the two pretrained checkpoints pinned in [collect.py](collect.py), and the repository's original external-real manifest and 24-item steering artifact. The recorded versions were NumPy 2.1.0, SciPy 1.18.0, PyArrow 24.0.0, PyTorch 2.12.0, Transformers 4.57.6, timesfm 2.0.2 and chronos-forecasting 2.3.1.

## Numerical and geometric audit of the atom experiment

An independent replay repeated the complete source-firing case layout, including repeated histories and the final partial batch, with a zero edit on both frozen models. Across 2,992 evaluation model–case pairs, the maximum absolute spurious forecast change was 2.15×10⁻⁶, the largest case-level RMS was 1.26×10⁻⁶, and the smallest scored atom-level RMS for a native or dense-mapped effect was .00190. Every individual tested effect exceeded ten times its corresponding replay RMS. The original atom inclusion floor of 10⁻⁵ passes this test. [Bolt replay](atom_audit_bolt.json) · [TimesFM replay](atom_audit_timesfm.json)

For the 1,024 clean histories, Bolt’s centered training matrix has full numerical column rank (512); its 16 decoder atoms lie in the observed row span. Yet ridge regularization retains only .181–.626 of their original norms (median .255). For TimesFM the corresponding row rank is 1,023 of 1,280, observed-span norm fractions are .916–.978 (median .967), and ridge-retained fractions are .355–.699 (median .526). These results distinguish the absence of variation in a direction from a deliberately regularized fit that attenuates observed directions. The numerical rank cutoff is 10⁻⁷ times the leading singular value. To reproduce both audits following atom material collection, run `python research/activation_transfer/controls/atom_audit.py bolt` and then the same command with `timesfm`. [Audit implementation](atom_audit.py)

## Learned and randomized target dictionaries

Sixteen clean PCA axes per source direction recovered the receiving dense intervention better than sixteen learned SAE atoms in all four cases. The learned target atoms beat a randomized version preserving their pre-normalization mean and covariance in the **Bolt** recipient, and scored below that control in **TimesFM**. Individual one-axis approximations could severely distort the forecast; the full reconstruction budget mattered.
