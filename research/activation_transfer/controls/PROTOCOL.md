# A black-box reference for transfer of forecast effects

Protocol frozen before running either forecaster on these histories, 2026-11-20 17:26 UTC. This is an independent evaluation within the activation-transfer study.

## Question

If two systems respond similarly to a shared input perturbation, how much of their agreement persists after allowing for the average effect of each waveform, simple characteristics of the original history, the scale of the input, and the identity of the series? This establishes a behavioral comparison for the hidden-intervention experiment. Its predictor is allowed to observe the source forecaster's response on the test history. Its training uses destination forecast labels. The hidden-transport experiment has different information at its disposal.

## Input changes and outputs

Standardize each history once using its unperturbed mean and population standard deviation. Query both pretrained forecasters, TimesFM 2.5 200M and Chronos-Bolt-small, using their native median at horizon 16. Retain the vector of signed central changes, (forecast(plus) − forecast(minus))/2, in clean-history standard deviations. Use the transport study's four final-patch inputs with amplitude ±0.35: linear ramp from −1 to +1; sine of period 8 at phase 0; step on the last 8 observations; triangular bump centered at offset 16 with half-width 4. The ramp, wave and step change the last observation. The bump preserves it.

Two diagnostic edits expose shortcuts. Translate all 128 points by ±0.35 to test forecast-level equivariance. In an additional constrained edit, permute no values: rotate the centered 30 interior values of the last patch by angles ±atan(0.35) toward a fixed dipole orthogonalized against the local constant and centered history. Both edited histories preserve the final observation, mean and variance of the whole series. The replacement leaves the first and last point of that patch intact. Normalize responses to this last diagnostic by the per-case input RMS difference, measured against the full 128 points, to remove a common variable-dose effect. Mark zero-dose cases and exclude those cases from this diagnostic only.

## Frozen predictors and comparisons

For each stimulus and destination model, fit a ridge regression from ten clean-history summaries (last value, last increment, recent linear slopes over 8 and 32 steps, recent-to-global scale, lag-1, lag-8 and lag-24 autocorrelation, mean absolute first difference and mean absolute second difference). Standardize inputs on daily calibration, fit an intercept and use ridge penalty n/10 on the standardized design. The same regression predicts the source response. Using only those calibration residuals, fit one nonnegative scalar per stimulus to map source residual trajectories to destination residual trajectories by least squares. The resulting test predictor adds this scaled source residual to the clean-input prediction of the destination response. A mean-only template and an analogous source correction supply a simpler baseline.

Primary endpoint for each direction and held-out set is the improvement over the clean-input predictor, computed as the unweighted average over the four original stimuli of 1 − SSE(augmented)/SSE(clean-input). Positive score means the source forecast movements carry some information above that baseline. Report both the point estimate and a 95% paired bootstrap interval over whole series (2,000 draws). For a donor null, shuffle whole source residual profiles jointly across all stimuli, within groups defined by quartiles of mean absolute first difference on the held-out set. Such shuffles preserve stimulus and broad roughness. Compute a one-sided permutation test over 1,999 permutations and Holm-adjust the six tests. The declared criterion for case-specific transfer is positive held-out gain and Holm p below .05. Report centered-horizon shape and level-only analyses separately, plus each stimulus and the two diagnostic stimuli; these are descriptive. Refit scalar and input predictors for the decomposed response. In a sensitivity check weight every evaluation history equally after converting each series' loss difference to a paired win; include ties as half wins.

## Interpretation

Shared neurons and shared internal dictionaries do not enter this calculation. Positive results establish how much paired response is available from the two output functions under the same external stimulus. A control that wins by extrapolating an average change or a common persistence shortcut has limited power to distinguish internal mechanisms. Negative results limit this simple transport rule on these doses, horizons and checkpoints. Lift is a prediction-of-response metric and says nothing about forecast accuracy against future observations.
