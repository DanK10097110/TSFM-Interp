# What MOMENT and cross-dictionary studies already established

The literature establishes several parts of this experiment. The specific open question here is whether an intervention selected in one sparse dictionary reproduces a measured, time-resolved forecasting effect in another dictionary or checkpoint, on the same series and on leads withheld from the controller. A second question is how much of that agreement survives a different forecast head, forecast request or choice of receiving token.

Time2time also tested event-statistic transplantation in two TSFM architectures; it is another reason to describe the gap precisely. The more restricted question in this repository conditions on the actual finite removal of a naturally firing source feature, compares the receiving edit to simple and matched controls and withholds forecast leads. We found no exact precedent for this full set of TSFM comparisons in the primary sources checked, which is weaker than a priority claim. [Time2time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05801) · [Earlier repository literature review](../GAP.md)

Liu and Xie compared five SAE designs over three seeds on frozen PatchTST and explicitly separated reconstruction fidelity, temporal coherence and physical calibration. Yıldırım found that ablating dominant latents in PatchTST feed-forward activations produced little forecast change in the settings studied. Different SAEs, checkpoints, tasks and readouts can support materially different conclusions. Our outcome matches a **specific measured finite forecast response**, and the group comparison uses a separate dictionary's physical write. Chen et al. previously transferred affine features, SAEs, probes and steering vectors across language models, so general priority for activation alignment or steering-vector transport would be unwarranted. [Structured sparse time-series autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04925) · [Superposition Is Not Necessary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05151) · [Transferring Features Across Language Models With Model Stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06609)

## Closest precedents for the group and input-edit results

Gerasimov et al. showed that individual unstable language-model SAE atoms can occupy reproducible lower-dimensional subspaces across seeds. Their cross-seed subspace test projects decoder matrices onto singular subspaces, and a synthetic experiment recovers a shared rank-two space while losing individual atoms. Paulo and Belrose had already shown seed dependence on the same model and training examples. Either finding makes a broad claim that “groups are more stable than features” unsuitable as a novelty claim here. Our narrower measurements ask whether a fixed signed sparse approximation can reproduce the *finite, time-resolved change in the native forecast*, whether a receiver can supply its own dose, and what happens when the head or checkpoint changes. [Gerasimov et al., sections 6 and F](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12138v1) · [Paulo and Belrose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16615)

Jander et al. compared how Chronos-2 and TimesFM-2.5 respond to interventions on six synthetic time-series generators, including trend and harmonic patterns. Guo et al. studied why observational feature-discovery scores fail to identify intervention attribution in language models and tested gradient-based predictions. The present temperature experiment combines observable edits with a different measurement: the receiver's independently observed middle-token change provides the target for another model's transported hidden edit. That distinction matters even when the end-to-end forecasts differ in magnitude. [Causal analysis of TSFM input patterns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24303) · [Observational discovery and intervention attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15850)
