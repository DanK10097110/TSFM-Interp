# The forecast readout changes the effect of an otherwise identical SAE feature

We saved a native middle-layer feature removal once, kept the encoder, input, feature ID, firing coefficient, token and all resulting hidden changes fixed, and evaluated five final linear heads. The hidden intervention is physically identical under all five heads. The first eight forecast values could respond differently enough that the published-head change was a poor prediction of the interior-trained-head change. This happened even on large, whose public decoder passes ordinary reconstruction checks.

| Checkpoint and domain | Frequent atoms / events, seed 1 | Published effect scored against the interior head | Half 1 effect scored against half 2 | Local derivative scored against its own published effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small, M4 daily | 13 / 276 | −1.64 | .18 | .98 |
| Small, hourly traffic | 4 / 253 | −1.42 | .60 | .98 |
| Large, M4 daily | 14 / 322 | −1.38 | .05 | .97 |
| Large, hourly traffic | 6 / 536 | −3.11 | −.20 | .96 |

The scores are equal-feature mean centered recovery of physical first-eight removal waveforms. An unchanged forecast scores zero and exact prediction one. Independently installed linear layers and full native forwards reproduced the archived effects, including those from replacement heads. On the second SAE seed, daily published-versus-interior recovery was −1.70 on small and −3.99 on large. Large traffic had six eligible atoms in both seeds, and its absolute amplitudes and per-feature results matter when interpreting large negative ratios. Forecasts under different readouts can have different accuracy even before any edit. **Ordinary accuracy is an incomplete test of causal agreement.** Two fits of the same small decoder, using independent 512-series training halves, had daily H16 MASE1 of 3.62 and 3.52. Their paired error difference was .094 [−.045, .223], and the two ordinary predictions differed by .074 history SD RMS. When a feature fired, one half's centered early response achieved .18 recovery of the other's response with the first SAE seed. In large, the half fits differed in ordinary daily accuracy (8.52 vs 9.38). Their traffic errors were closer (1.73 vs 1.71), but their first-seed feature-effect recovery there was −.20. These are descriptive error matches and their ordinary forecasts are distinct. [Small ordinary forecasts](results/small_ordinary.json) · [Large ordinary forecasts](results/large_ordinary.json)

**How repeatable is the split result?** Eight alternative complementary training splits give a symmetric comparison that treats both heads equally. On daily M4, across splits, median early agreement of the two large readouts on feature displacements was .02 and −.17 for the two SAE seeds. Replacing each displacement by an equally long difference between two randomly paired natural forecast states gave .69 and .58. Nearest input-history controls gave .30 and .29; isotropic vectors gave −.54 and −.52. Small's two feature-removal scores were .30 and .33. The stronger contrast in large is consistent with linear heads being more tightly constrained along directions explored by ordinary series differences. The fraction of hidden energy covered by the top 32 training PCs did not give a single ordering for every seed, and the training folds overlap across repetitions. These are finite trained-head and domain sensitivities. The receiver can only implement the behavior requested by a particular readout. With the first small SAE seed and Bolt as recipient on daily series, a controller informed by the published head reached .85 against the published effect. The interior-trained head's own controller reached .95 against its effect; applying the published controller and scoring it against the interior effect gave −1.31. Each used the same receiving derivative and hidden norm ceiling. The first eight ordinary source derivatives were estimated with small signed probes and the actual finite feature removal was held out. [Cross-model results](results/small_22112201_to_bolt.json) · [Measured physical waveforms](results/small_22112201_to_bolt.npz)

These experiments identify the dependence of forecast attribution on a particular downstream task head. The linear identity Δforecast = Δfinal-state × decoder × scale explains why the dependence is possible; its size across actual naturally active features, independently trained heads and real histories is the empirical result. No semantic label was assigned to the features.
