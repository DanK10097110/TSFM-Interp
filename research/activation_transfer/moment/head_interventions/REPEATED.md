# Is low agreement between two independently fitted decoders specific to SAE directions?

After the first two small-model heads disagreed on several finite feature removals, repeat the split rather than interpreting a single fit. Build eight prespecified complementary random partitions of the same 1,024 daily training series (512 per head); use the same interior reconstruction objective and the original independent 128-series penalty-validation procedure for all sixteen heads. Across repeats training sets overlap, so report their range and median as split sensitivity, with no claim of sixteen independent experiments. Evaluate on the fixed daily and traffic panels and both SAE dictionary seeds for small and large. Use only pointwise linear operations on previously measured real hidden differences and independently verify representative head substitution on native model passes.

Compare output disagreement on three types of matched-length hidden displacement at the final pair of future mask states: (1) the actual finite displacement caused by a middle-layer feature removal, (2) the difference between the ordinary output states of two different real histories in the same domain, and (3) an isotropic random displacement. Use both the nearest distinct original input history by Euclidean distance on 480 normalized values and a fixed derangement of original input histories. Scale each control to exactly the same joint hidden norm as its corresponding feature displacement. All pairings are frozen independently of readout outputs. Use the signed, horizon-centered eight-lead waveform, independently at both patches; a single scalar head intercept cancels for displacements. For each feature and split calculate symmetric disagreement by dividing squared head-to-head differences by the average squared magnitude of the two outputs. Also report absolute RMS in history-SD units and response amplitudes. A low score can arise from weak output projection even for an accurately measured, reproducible hidden change.

To probe the explanation, compare the fraction of each displacement's squared hidden norm in the leading training covariance eigenvectors (ranks 8, 32, 128) of the interior mask-state regression design. This is a description of support in the training design, not an identification theorem. If natural and SAE differences are both unstable, or if covered variance does not distinguish them, the specific “unusual edit direction” explanation is weakened.
