# Which path carries a recent temperature change to the forecast?

Use all 46 spatially separated USCRN stations and their four frozen seasonal forecast dates in 2026. In Celsius, add 2 to the entire history (change of origin), add 2 to its most recent eight samples, or add a linear ramp from −2 to +2 over just those eight samples. Forecast H16. Compare the released small and large MOMENT models. On the identical modified observations also record Bolt and TimesFM, plus the literal last-observation rule, without fitting a new model. A hypothesized sensor/history edit is a behavioral counterfactual, not a real intervention on weather.

Decompose each observed-input effect into three mediators of the released masked reconstruction model: middle-block observed patch states, middle-block future mask states, and its final reverse-normalization factor (both shift and scale). Capture the original/modified histories' middle states and RevIN affine factors. On the original public graph replace the observed or future middle token group, neither or both. Measure the ordinary full normalized and denormalized outputs. The ignored left padding must be causally masked. Swapping both groups and the final affine factor should reproduce the actual modified forecast. Replay the equivalent input/target-statistic replacement on a sample with live hooks as a unit check. Compute Shapley contributions over the eight combinations, reporting complete waveform/replay residuals, Celsius amplitude, normalized shares, and station-blocked intervals. Global additive shift is the planted control: its reverse normalization must recover nearly all of its own shift with almost no hidden change. A local perturbation has a smaller mean shift and can move both observed and future hidden states. Measure the signs and magnitudes on leads 1–8 and 9–16, without assuming they must trace the same path across scales. Use the station as independent sampling block and show waveforms per lead. Record cross-model agreement, and distinguish level from horizon-centered shape and signed gain from squared-error skill.
