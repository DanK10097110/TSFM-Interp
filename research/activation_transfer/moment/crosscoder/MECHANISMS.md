# What changes when we change the measurement?

## An input intervention that bypasses the linear probes

On 64 M4 daily and 64 traffic histories, we physically changed only the last eight observations. Seven orthogonal, zero-mean patterns span all variations of that patch at fixed level. For each feature, the seven measured changes in hidden activation along its decoder direction give a sensitivity profile. We compared the two models' profiles for a paired coordinate with the profiles obtained from the other 31 source-active coordinates. No patch probe or forecast head enters this test. Across two paired training methods, two seeds and two domains, the matched advantage was **.199–.303** at a .15 history-SD edit and **.171–.277** at half that dose. All paired history/feature intervals were positive. All corresponding shuffled-training intervals included zero. The dense ordinary-state map had slightly higher absolute alignment in all eight primary cells. These are signed responses to changes in temporal order within an observed patch. [Native effects and dose dependence](input_sensitivity/README.md)

Level agreement depends on which quantity we call “level.” With correctly paired masked MOMENT crosscoders on M4 daily, the decoded **observed patch mean** moved in the same direction 96.6%/94.1% of the time. For the mean of the **native 16-step forecast change**, the same-sign rates were 54.5%/53.9%, just 2.2/1.4 percentage points above the within-history active comparator. An earlier transport result for a deliberately constructed level edit is compatible with these measurements on naturally chosen crosscoder coordinates. ## The same final state supports different readouts

In the published MOMENT short-forecast call, the last observed patch occupies slot 61. Slots 62 and 63 produce the future. We also measured neighboring observed slots and moved the edit one patch earlier. Simply inspecting the native readout at the edited observed slot did little to rescue cross-model agreement. The new 2025 USCRN panel repeated this measurement on 24 stations and 12 forecast dates. Average edited-slot matched-minus-distractor cosine was −.018 [−.097, .063] for the ordinary joint crosscoder and −.043 [−.111, .025] with masking. Real effects reached .15–.17°C RMS in MOMENT-small and .04–.05°C in MOMENT-large at the edited slot. [Frozen native-site design](READOUT_PROTOCOL.md) · [Fresh dates, physical scales and controls](fresh_readout/README.md)

We traced identical finite middle-block perturbations to the edited token at three depths. At each depth we fit a linear readout of the same eight observed values on M4. At the final layer each model's native reconstruction head and its external probe saw the **identical clean and edited hidden tensors**. On the fixed daily sample the final external readout increased matched-minus-distractor agreement above the native readout by .837/.382 in the two joint fits and .694/.565 in the masked fits. On traffic the increases were .322/.691 and .829/.374. These are 32 source-active edits per domain and seed. Some shared information about the observed patch survived to the final layer. Which output we read at that layer materially changed the correspondence we measured. The probes were fit on an explicit common task. The smaller model's released reconstruction head also has the orthogonal row geometry and zero bias previously measured in this repo. This comparison supports a readout contribution at the edited observed token; differences in propagation to the future slots may also matter. On the 288 weather histories the same readout substitution produced a .634 [.529, .736] pairing-advantage increase averaged across the two masked seeds. The history-shuffled fits gained only .044 [−.097, .190] from that substitution. The paired difference between these gains was .590 [.423, .761]. The final external probes read the ordinary within-patch temporal shape with skill .719 for small and .817 for large relative to predicting zero centered shape. The published heads gave −2.786 and .025 on that same shape task. Context overlap across a station's dates is reflected in the station/date bootstrap. ## Can historical forecast responses identify exceptions?

For the two masked MOMENT dictionaries we used 96 separate M4 histories with measured derivatives to choose eight coordinates whose first-eight forecast shapes aligned best between models. We excluded both evaluation populations from all selection. On 128 held-out daily series their mean derivative cosine was **.295/.299**, versus −.014/−.003 across all eligible candidates. On the later eight leads the selected results fell to .003/.057. Repeating exactly the same search on 16 arbitrary target-coordinate reassignments yielded mean early cosine .335/.329. Thus some short-horizon alignments can be found prospectively, and this amount of success is achievable without the crosscoder's original pairings. Under actual natural doses and a training-calibrated receiving gain, early equal-feature waveform recovery on daily was .135/.063 for the true selections. The predesignated reassignment gave .124/.012. Few selected atoms fired on traffic and realized effects varied markedly. A restricted set with measurable alignment does not establish a broadly portable dictionary. ## Why did traffic give a larger forecast signal?

A plausible explanation is that both forecasters project the same daily traffic cycle forward. We kept the final 32 observations, historical values and selected directions fixed. Permuting 18 older days but retaining their hourly phase gave mean lag-24 correlation .557 in that segment. Rotating each of those day blocks independently reduced it to .001. Both models changed their ordinary forecasts. Nevertheless, the masked crosscoder's forecast-shape advantage was .200/.225 with phase preserved and .188/.169 with phase scrambled. The paired differences were .012 [−.088, .109] and .056 [−.014, .128]. The two ordinary joint fits also had intervals containing zero. Destroying that older rhythm left appreciable transfer on this panel. The final 32 hours still carry local structure, and scrambling historical block boundaries changes continuity. This evidence does not isolate one cause of the traffic–daily difference. ## Which target feature will fire?

The detector experiment fixes four previously nominated MOMENT-large SAE directions and predicts their dosage from the small model. In the best point-estimate condition, a small-to-large state map fit on M4 endpoint tokens first translates the ordinary hidden state, after which the original large SAE applies its own 4,096-way TopK competition. On weather, the inferred and actual TopK active sets overlapped by .867 versus .752 for always predicting the 32 most frequent training coordinates. Among the four studied features the map made 48 wrong decisions in 87 cases within 5% of the native threshold, versus 89 in 1,083 cases at least 50% away. Errors concentrate near the competition boundary. The native target's continuous state did not need to be reconstructed exactly to recover many of its winners. Sparse gate inference and recognizing the effect of a known direction are different goals. [State accuracy, all-coordinate overlap and gate-distance bins](results/mechanism.json)

The result depends on the population. Re-encoding the endpoint map gave four-feature dose skill .857 on held-out M4 and .660 on weather but −.118 on traffic. Training the state map on all patch positions raised traffic dose skill to .618 and reduced weather skill to .538. Two traffic features fired only three times and once, making equal-feature scores unstable. Across architectures, on NOAA the mapped state compared unfavorably with direct dose prediction for four frequent Bolt nominees, and the two methods were close for four TimesFM nominees. A three-layer small-model readout of the target gate gave .514 native weather waveform skill against .552 for the middle layer alone. This is a test of additional depth under a specified readout. The causal trace above gives a concrete criterion for a future multilayer SAE study: test whether a learned feature predicts where a shared input perturbation changes its downstream forecast effect, conditional on the current history, against a dense-map and a measurement-matched baseline.
