# Several receiving locations can reproduce the first eight days

A Chronos-Bolt feature removal on daily M4 produced an eight-step forecast change that we could reproduce inside **three different middle-layer token positions of MOMENT-large**. The corresponding later eight predictions differed markedly. If the first eight outputs were the only validation, all three writes could appear to be the same mechanism.

| Receiving location in MOMENT-large | Days 1–8 centered recovery | Days 9–16 centered recovery |
|---|---:|---:|
| Last observed token, middle block | .95 | −2.98 |
| First future placeholder, middle block | .97 | −1.39 |
| Second future placeholder, middle block | .93 | −9.35 |
| First future placeholder, terminal readout | .97 | .00 |

The source includes 15 sufficiently frequent atoms firing in 360 history/atom combinations across 128 daily histories. All rows used the ordinary-model source and receiving derivatives for days 1–8 and physically measured both models' edited forecasts. The column for days 9–16 was withheld. A score of zero corresponds to leaving those leads untouched. The small or large checkpoint receives historical inputs in units of each history's own standard deviation. Results under TimesFM donors and on traffic are in the linked archives, including the low-coverage two-atom TimesFM traffic case. **A known nonzero intervention identifies why these locations differ.** The terminal pointwise decoder applies the same eight-value readout to each token. With a fixed nonzero test vector at the terminal readout, writing the last observed token had exactly zero effect. Writing the first or second future token changed just its own block of eight, producing matching within-block patterns at the two locations up to 0.000001 history SD. The score of zero at later leads in the final table row therefore has a structural explanation. Earlier in the bidirectional encoder a token can still influence other positions. We then asked a more practical question. Can a controller change the early forecast and leave the later forecast alone? On a capped subset of 204 natural Bolt daily events, constraining the large middle first-token write to the current later derivative's null space reduced its actual late RMS from .00317 to .00014 history SD while keeping its early centered skill at .974. For the second token the late RMS fell from .00972 to .00085 and early skill moved from .914 to .900. Using the later derivative from another history instead left late RMS at .00266 and .00869. Late information was deliberately **available** to these guard constraints. Their objective is later invariance, distinct from predicting the donor's unobserved continuation. On small, guarding the second slot reduced the late RMS from .03191 to .00115, yet early centered skill remained poor (−.666 unguarded, −.698 guarded). Dose-matched and quarter-dose controls and per-donor data are included.
