# A 16-axis replacement survived two new receiver SAE fits

Across three independently initialized SAEs on the same frozen MOMENT-large encoder, a fixed group of 16 receiver decoder directions reproduced the donor’s physical forecast effect with centered 16-lead skill of **.806, .758 and .640**. Replacing the donor with the single receiver atom whose training activations best correlated with it gave **−.060, −4.228 and .075** at the receiver’s own natural dose. The group beat that single for 35 of the 36 donor/receiver combinations. The distinction matters for interpretation. The group reconstructs a known donor direction in a second dictionary and uses the donor’s measured amplitude on each history. [Prospective protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Per-donor results, absolute errors and intervals](results/summary.json)

The first receiver is the original comparison. Seeds 22112203 and 22112204 are two newly trained SAEs. All three were scored against the same archive of measured source-feature removals, on four 2026 dates at 46 US weather stations. The original 16 training-nominated donors produced 669 natural firings. Twelve donors fired on at least eight stations, giving 663 eligible events. The primary statistic measures reduction in squared error of the signed forecast-change waveform after removing its lead-wise mean and scaling by observed history SD. A score of 1 reproduces the original edit, and zero predicts no effect. Donors carry equal weight. [Original donor archive](../weather/results/22112201.npz) · [Interventions and design](METHODS.md)

Resampling these three fitted dictionaries would give little information about the wider population of possible receiver SAEs. [All paired intervals](results/summary.json)

**The negative average for seed 22112203 hides both successful matches and costly mistakes.** Its median donor scored .116 at the natural dose and .609 at matched norm. Donor 1867 and its matched receiver 466 co-fired in all 43 cases. Their decoder cosine was .226, and the native receiver write was 5.87 times larger at the median. The native substitute scored −42.15 for this donor. Matching its norm improved skill to −1.90, while the fixed group reached .545. Even matched firing and a corrected dose left a different forecast shape. The 16-axis rule also had a counterexample. Donor 2596 under receiver 22112204 scored −.774 with the group, −.436 with the natural single and −.383 with the equal-norm single. The group beat the equal-norm single for 34/36 donor/receiver combinations. Its median hidden-direction reconstruction R² across donors was .742, .671 and .692, respectively. Each group was constructed from receiver decoder rows on historical data. Only 93, 97 and 100 of the 4,096 receiver rows met the matching rule of eight positive activations on the 1,024 final training patches. This experiment expands sampling of receiver initializations for one donor dictionary, training corpus, site and encoder. It provides evidence for a reusable **constructed intervention**, and leaves the identity or meaning of the individual constituent features unresolved. Each new receiver ran 669 interventions under each of the three policies, totalling 4,014 forecast edits. A second implementation rebuilt selected writes from both SAE checkpoints and reran the entire encoder on individual raw histories. All 24 full-forward checks agreed within .00000382°C, and the independent final-head calculation agreed within .00000768°C over every new edit. The original receiver’s matched identities, 16-axis coefficients, three primary scores, station and crossed intervals, and pooled RMS reproduced the existing archive.
