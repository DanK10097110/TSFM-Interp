# What does a new history add to cross-checkpoint steering?

We tested whether a fixed steering direction could be learned from paired forecast sensitivities on previous histories. For each of 16 frozen donor SAE atoms, a dense direction or a signed group of 16 recipient SAE directions was fitted on 96 historical daily items, with 32 separate items selecting regularization. The fitting target was the directional derivative of the donor's first eight forecast leads along its signed decoder direction, measured on each unedited training history. On unseen daily and traffic histories we applied the resulting fixed vector at the actual donor dose and measured the change in a live H16 forecast. The reference was the donor's **actual finite removal**. Donor and receiving gradients from those unseen histories were reserved for the separately labeled adaptive policy. All columns through “same actual dose” report centered first-eight recovery. The final column scores the exact adaptive write on the withheld next eight. There were 14/6 eligible source features on daily/traffic for large and 13/4 for small. A history-shuffled fit used paired architectures but the wrong donor response histories, retained regularization selection and a matching geometric per-atom norm ceiling. The large negative small→large numbers are amplified by the weak source head's small effect size. The calibrated fixed policy avoids that oversteer, but its average recovered shape remains close to zero. A small→large benefit over its training-pairing sham on daily (+.025) has a crossed history/feature interval of approximately [.01, .05]. It is a detectable but small effect, distinct from the large fraction recovered within one encoder. **The equal-budget control changes the interpretation.** The adaptive and fixed policies share the same per-event norm ceiling, defined by the activation map and natural source dose, and share the same training-defined shape/level objective. Substituting the receiving derivative from a different history keeps the correct donor request and uses exactly the same actual length as the current-history write. The early current-minus-wrong gain was positive in both directions and both domains. The larger budget follow-up allowed a fixed direction four times the original norm. Large→small daily early skill worsened from −.061 to −.577; in small→large the original fixed fit was already well inside its limit and the score stayed at .030. The weaker numbers in the table versus some unconstrained original controls also involve a different ridge objective and shape weighting (.226 for a small source, 1.728 for a large source). Only within-protocol comparisons isolate the changed factor. At fixed dose the current receiving derivative helped predict which directions control this particular history's near-term response. Several other histories still offered usable early directions, especially in traffic. The same contrasts offered no robust late continuation and some became strongly negative. That is evidence for context-dependent **local functional control**. Other representation maps, forecast horizons, task heads and training distributions could support other forms of reuse.

Independent direct additive hooks verified the calibration derivatives against central finite differences at eight separate items per checkpoint, including held-out regularization cases. Relative directional error at step .10 on the chosen donor directions was .00057 for large and .0058 for small. On nine fresh event rows in each transfer direction, the three fixed-policy predictions agreed with a live additive full-graph replay within .00002 history SD.
