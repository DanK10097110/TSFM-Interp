# Can historical intervention effects train a reusable cross-checkpoint direction?

After seeing the weak ordinary-state mapping from large into small and the strong within-small multi-atom result, test an output-based alternative. Use a single fixed direction per source atom that can be reused at test time without computing a derivative of either model. For the first preselected SAE seed in both small→large and large→small directions, choose 128 paired M4 fit histories deterministically from the original 1,024 SAE fitting histories; reserve 96 to estimate the mapping and 32 to select a shared regularization penalty. None belongs to the daily/traffic effect evaluation. H16, middle block, last observed patch, native published heads, natural source feature coefficients and ordinary input scaling stay as in the direct comparison.

For each of the 16 predetermined donor atoms compute the ordinary derivative of its first eight forecast values at a unit direction. Stack recipient first-eight derivatives over the same training histories. Learn (1) a single dense ridge direction for that atom; (2) a fixed signed group of sixteen receiver SAE decoder directions, selected greedily in the space of measured forecast responses rather than hidden coordinates, followed by a bounded refit; (3) a dense sham after permuting the training histories of the donor responses. Keep each direction's maximum hidden norm equal to the earlier geometric map of this source atom. Use a common penalty chosen on the 32 validation histories, with separate selection for the dense sham. In all prediction arms multiply by the actual source firing coefficient and measure a real receiving-model forecast. No test response or receiving gradient enters these fixed policies. At test compare the centered early donor waveform and the unmeasured next eight to the original state-only map, geometric signed group, no intervention, and the much more informed current-history derivative controller. Mean/full scores and single-atom outcomes are secondary. This is targeted output calibration and can at most establish task- and model-pair-specific reuse.
