# Can a fixed group of recipient SAE features recover a single donor intervention?

The within-small single-match intervention is often worse than no edit. Test a different explanation: independent dictionaries split the original hidden direction across several features. A sparse approximation can be fitted once per original atom and reused on every new series without asking for any new recipient derivative. Use M4-trained matches, candidate IDs with at least eight last-token firings on 1,024 fitting items (as in the single match). Regress the source decoder vector, or its fitted small↔large geometric transport, on the second SAE's unit decoder rows. Use orthogonal matching pursuit with free signed coefficients (choose maximum absolute residual dot product, refit the selected rows by least squares), at group sizes 1, 4, and 16. The single projected atom may have negative coefficient; the earlier single positive-cosine, equal-norm arm remains a comparator. Publish the IDs, weights, overlap, training support and hidden approximation. No evaluation activations, outputs, labels or forecast directions enter selection of group or coefficients.

Apply each fixed signed group multiplied by the donor's actual natural coefficient; it need not align with the recipient's current support. To distinguish geometric reuse from natural simultaneous activity, also test the 16-feature group after zeroing its terms whose recipient feature is inactive on the individual history. The norm drops in this arm; use a fixed-group direction scaled to the same remaining physical norm as a dosage control. Use the exact same natural fires as the single-match tests. Evaluate early and later actual forecast effects, with the same whole-history and crossed-feature intervals and model-scale qualifications. For computation, preregister both donor seeds for small→small and large→large and the first donor seed in each cross-size direction. A same-encoder gain with group size supports a distributed correspondence under this metric; evidence for literal equivalence of individual concepts requires more.
