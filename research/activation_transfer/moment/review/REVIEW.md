# Independent review of the MOMENT transfer claims

The archived interventions support strong substitution of a donor write by a signed group from a second dictionary **in the same frozen MOMENT-large encoder**. Across model sizes, current forecast derivatives support early output control, and transferring the original hidden direction performs poorly. Two qualifications matter for a paper. Individual matched features reproduce some of the larger effects, and an early controller receives considerably more information than a state-only geometric map. [Independent computations](results/review.json) · [Checker](check.py)

## Same encoder, two dictionaries

I found zero duplicate series IDs or standardized input-window hashes between the 1,024 fitting histories and 256 daily/traffic evaluation histories. I rebuilt the last-observed-patch TopK codes, historical coactivation matches, source-firing rows, recipient decoder mixtures and station/origin joins. Both large SAEs saw the same ordinary training examples. On 46 weather stations × four origins, the two source directions have 12/16 and 15/16 atoms firing in at least eight distinct stations, contributing 663 and 484 scored events. Of these, 239 and 205 also had their matched recipient atom naturally open. The score includes the closed recipients, whose own-dose forecasts receive a zero write. The group uses the actual donor coefficient and can write signed terms through closed recipient features. [Intervention and eligibility rules](../dictionary_transfer/weather/PROTOCOL.md)

The primary score averages each atom’s squared-error skill. Pooling errors and donor energies across events instead gives greater weight to large effects. Repeating the calculation in raw degrees Celsius yields:

| Native H16 weather outcome, in °C | SAE 1 → 2 group / natural single | SAE 2 → 1 group / natural single |
|---|---:|---:|
| Centered waveform, equal-atom skill | .809 / −.086 | .788 / −1.547 |
| Centered waveform, energy-weighted skill | .905 / .123 | .918 / .129 |
| Full waveform, energy-weighted skill | .918 / .321 | .932 / .330 |
| Centered mean absolute error | .0130 / .0374°C | .0120 / .0363°C |
| Full mean absolute error | .0220 / .0599°C | .0250 / .0770°C |

The raw-Celsius centered group advantage over the own-dose single was .783 [.708, .858] and .789 [.640, .942] under energy weighting and 3,000 paired, whole-station resamples. Groups also beat the forced equal-dose singles and the closest individual decoder on pooled squared and absolute error. Group centered RMSE was .0210/.0186°C, against donor centered RMS .0682/.0651°C. The original history-SD equal-atom values reproduce at .806/.786. **“About 80%” refers to the average atom’s reduction in squared error.** The pooled energy reduction is closer to 90%. The three highest-energy donors account for 64% and 58% of centered Celsius energy in the two directions. Natural singles have positive pooled point estimates even when their macro-average is negative. Mean signed bias can also favor a single by cancellation. The group result establishes a forced reconstruction, and the weather arms alone do not establish the specificity of the trained group versus other 16-dimensional bases. The separate daily/traffic run includes rotated-dictionary and PCA controls. The result survives effect weighting, but the scale of the improvement varies. On daily small → large, the donor effect had pooled early RMS .00256 history SD and the dense mapped target effect .01182. On reversal the corresponding values were .00869 and .00242. The output scales help explain the asymmetric losses. Daily large → small early adaptive skill drops from .719 per atom to .410 when pooled. The derivative controller uses the donor’s current coefficient and ordinary first-eight recipient **and donor** forecast derivatives. I independently recovered the saved goals and the norms of representative capped writes by SVD. No subsequent observed value or donor finite knockout output enters this solve. “Late” means the unrequested last eight **outputs of the same H16 computation**. Both native placeholder tokens are present when measuring the first eight derivatives. The paired controls establish information about the current model response beyond write length. These are output-control results for the specified horizon, patched site, donors and checkpoints. They supply little late recovery, and the small checkpoint’s unusual published reconstruction head limits generalization to capable forecasters. The review covers all eight registered baseline comparisons and all six registered group comparisons. Groups include both within-size seeds and one cross-size seed per direction. ## Post-release targets and archive integrity

I checked every one of the 2,944 scored weather temperatures against field 9 of the dated NOAA ZIP, all 88,100 observed input entries, and all 220 missing input replacements. An independent reconstruction using 2008–2023 calendar bins and past anomalies reproduced the imputations, climatology and climate-plus-anomaly forecasts to 1.5×10⁻¹⁴°C. MAE was 5.661°C for large, 5.097°C for persistence, 3.654°C for climate plus anomaly, 3.702°C for Bolt and 3.873°C for TimesFM. The forecast-quality panel’s public large outputs and the separate weather intervention ordinary forecasts agreed within 7.63×10⁻⁶°C. Each physically measured candidate effect and its float64 readout check agreed within 8.01×10⁻⁶°C. [Frozen sample and dates](../forecast_quality/post_release/PROTOCOL.md) · [Raw archival results](results/review.json)

**Every forecast target is post-release. The information boundary is per origin.** All five local safetensors matched the publishers’ SHA-256 values and all configurations matched their git blobs at the recorded Hub revisions. The three MOMENT revisions date to March 26, TimesFM to October 2 and Bolt to November 21, 2025. [Publisher hashes, dates and exact source URLs](results/publisher_revisions.json) Each forecast’s inputs end the preceding date. Of the 64 unique target days per station, 48 appear as valid observations in a later origin’s history. Station eligibility requires complete future measurements. The code checks their availability. The same four dates and geographically separated stations recur across methods. Station intervals preserve repeated dates per site, but cannot measure variation across years, remove residual spatial dependence, or substitute for a second prospectively collected panel. The intervention endpoint uses donor and recipient model predictions, while forecasting MAE uses the true temperatures.

The checker imports no project scoring or intervention functions. It independently starts from released archive tensors and stored finite waveforms, hashes the inputs, and checks joins, selection, scales and denominators. This is an independent arithmetic, artifact and information-flow audit of existing model runs. A synthetic test with unequal donor energies yielded the known skills .500, .941 and .800 for equal-atom, pooled squared and pooled absolute error. It failed when a copy of the implementation substituted equal-atom skill for pooled energy. Run `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m research.activation_transfer.moment.review.check` at repo root with the original scratch caches and official NOAA ZIP available.
