# MOMENT forecast quality on actual observed futures

Frozen November 22, 2026 before selecting items, computing forecasts or scoring outcomes. The question is whether the official pretrained MOMENT reconstruction head, used for zero-shot short forecasting, is a credible forecast-producing backbone for the project's eight- and sixteen-lead intervention tasks.

**Data and information boundary.** Use six public archives already available locally: GiftEval M4 daily (weekly reference period 7), M4 weekly (52), Bitbrains random-storage hourly VM channel 0 (24, alternative 168), Chronos M4 hourly (24, 168), Monash traffic hourly (24, 168) and Monash electricity hourly (24, 168). Use only the raw numeric series and source-declared frequency, with 480 sequential observations at each origin. Exclude the repository's named external-real benchmark items from their corresponding domain, deduplicate exact context bytes, and require finite histories, finite next 16 values, and nonconstant histories for scale estimation. No screen on the size, sign, variability or predictability of the future. Order item IDs by a fixed SHA-256 salt; the first 24 qualifying series in each domain are diagnostic, the next up to 96 comprise the primary holdout. Record any shortfall. The model weights stay frozen and we do not fit a readout or tune a model on the primary histories or futures. Public model pretraining exposure cannot be certified from these archives.

Use two observed forecast origins per series. Choose offsets from the final available origin by an ID-hashed RNG without looking at values. Offset range is 56 observations for daily, 13 for weekly and 168 for hourly frequency; ensure the earlier origin precedes the later one by at least 16 observations and each has 480 available trailing observations. An earlier test can subsequently become part of the later *observed* context. All uncertainty calculations keep both origins together within their series. Store item IDs, source hashes, raw indices and the 16 real future values with the histories. Models receive only the 480 observed values. Every scaling statistic and classical method including method selection uses only the current history.

**Methods.** Use pinned Chronos-Bolt-small and TimesFM-2.5-200m medians plus the official pretrained MOMENT-1-small reconstruction head in native zero-shot short_forecast mode (no supervised or random forecasting head). Run separate natural H=8 and H=16 forecast calls for each model; expose and quantify any discrepancy over their shared first eight leads. Feed raw observations, and as a separate prespecified arm repeat all three models with external per-history z-scoring and invert on the way out. No test future chooses the scaling. Always retain forecasts in original series units. If the official larger MOMENT release becomes available from the shared checkpoint loader evaluate it on this fixed panel with the same procedure; report runtime and checkpoint identity. Baselines include last-value, end-to-end drift, primary-period seasonal naive, median of up to four prior cycles, and the equivalent alternatives at a second period for hourly data. A history-only selector picks, per origin and horizon, the minimum historical MAE method across the last eight complete nonoverlapping horizon-length backtests. No future or cross-series fit enters this selection.

**Estimands.** Primary outcomes are direct H=8 and direct H=16 mean absolute error divided by the history's mean absolute first difference (MASE1). Average the two origins per series. Within each domain report mean MASE1, raw mean absolute error and RMSE, error divided by the history SD, and the median across series. Absolute metrics aggregate over different native scales and are descriptive within domain. Primary cross-domain comparison is the equal-domain mean of ratios of mean MASE1 between a model and its paired comparator. Also report per-domain ratios, fractional series wins (half credit on ties), and paired 95% percentile intervals from 4,000 resamples of series independently per domain, sharing draws across every method. These intervals describe this panel conditional on domains and pretrained checkpoints. Distinguish skill against last value, history-only selected baseline, and the other foundation forecasters. Score leads 9–16 separately from the shared early subset as a secondary. Avoid using a high overall score to declare a universal winner.

**Normalization and batch verification.** On a preordered subset of up to eight diagnostic items per domain, inspect positive affine equivariance under moderate positive scale/shift, very small and very large units, mirrored trajectories separately, constancy, single versus mixed batches and order, native command versus explicit external history standardization. Compare H=8 and H=16 separately. Use original-unit discrepancies normalized by the old history standard deviation, count failed tolerances and disclose the effective amplitude relative to implementation epsilons. Confirm a raw source-based replay and reproduce a subset of model calls separately from the benchmark driver. Synthetic controls have known invariants and any new test is mutation-checked in isolation.

The study is a short-horizon evaluation of a particular public inference procedure. Forecast quality, causal interpretability and public pretraining provenance are distinct measurements.
