# Can a forecast intervention transfer between time-series foundation models?

*Study opened 20 November 2026.*

**Paired-dictionary follow-up.** On held-out M4 and traffic data we can now distinguish a shared representation of the observed patch from a shared use of that representation in the next forecast. Crosscoders trained on paired MOMENT states and paired TimesFM–Chronos-Bolt states recovered agreements in the time-varying input shape after removing its level. Their native forecast effects agreed much less. The across-family traffic result is a positive exception in forecast direction, with weak source-dose waveform recovery. The remaining gap is how to identify a direction and gain that reproduce a forecast change in a new context without observing that context's target response. [Paired input and forecast experiments](moment/crosscoder/README.md) · [Measured exceptions and explanatory controls](moment/crosscoder/MECHANISMS.md)

**22 November update.** Published studies already demonstrate MOMENT SAE steering, cross-model TSFM representation analysis, and causal correspondence tests for language-model SAEs. An October public report also predates our small/base reconstruction-head diagnosis. The new experiments isolate which head, forecast token, dictionary and receiving derivative are needed to reproduce a finite donor intervention. [Primary-source review](moment/RELATED.md) · [Experiments](moment/README.md)

The current evidence sharpens the gap. Language-model research already showed that individual SAE features vary across seeds while some lower-dimensional spaces reproduce. Within three forecasting architectures we can now show how well a **particular removal's entire forecast waveform** survives an independently chosen single atom or a sparse group, how much depends on knowing the donor dose, and how small that effect can be in the original units. Across architectures, a fixed map can predict much of the receiver's reaction to a defined input edit, while a generic historical direction reproduces a large fraction and precise interior order contributes less. Which part of a shared response can be attributed to the current history and to a particular internal direction remains a narrower open question. [Closest subspace precedent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12138) · [Independent SAE seeds](moment/dictionary_transfer/more_seeds/README.md) · [Equal-angle alternatives](moment/dictionary_transfer/angle_control/README.md) · [Physical edit and temporal-order test](shape_transfer/physical_ramp/new_dates/order/README.md)

An SAE feature can recognize the same histories in two forecasters while editing each feature changes the forecasts differently. We want to predict the receiving model’s signed forecast response to a transferred edit on a new series. The scientific question is what correspondence, if any, survives a change of checkpoint, architecture and input series.

The existing seven-model panel reproduced all 20 selected shared-input claims on held-out histories and on 800 external windows. In one private evaluation, ten of 31 within-model feature effects reproduced, while zero of six testable shared-input causal comparisons passed the registered criterion. The original agreement test also missed some planted effects. Counting its failures as evidence that mechanisms differ would overstate the experiment’s sensitivity. The existing dense ridge stitching predicts hidden states across models, which leaves open how the recipient uses a *change* in those states. [Held-out effects](../../FINDINGS.md#ca-11--held-out-10-of-31-single-feature-causal-claims-confirm-no-cross-model-causal-claim-does) · [Previous experiments and controls](../../FINDINGS.md)

Other groups found steering vectors that add a trend or periodicity to outputs, layer-dependent sensitivity to ablation, and a gap between recovering a known trend slope from a hidden state and obtaining the expected forecast change. A Chronos SAE study found strong differences across encoder depths, but its stated ablation baseline combines removal of a feature with passage through the SAE. Together these motivate interventions on the actual receiving model, an intact-state baseline, and controls for what the original input already predicts. [Wiliński et al., ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wilinski25a.html) · [Bao et al., ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/bao26c.html) · [Yang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34316v1) · [Mishra, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10071v1)

## Competing explanations

1. **The active SAE features change abruptly.** A slight change to a state can move two near-tied features across the TopK threshold. A change of identity need not matter if their decoder vectors make similar contributions. Measure support changes, decoded movement and the original dense movement on the same perturbation.
2. **The useful coordinate systems differ.** Centred paired hidden states provide observations from which a linear map can learn a correspondence. Applying its linear part to a source edit is a test on displacements. Measure success by actually applying that displacement to the recipient and comparing its forecast with the recipient’s own independently measured edit.
3. **The models read similar information differently.** A transferred edit might match the recipient’s native effect and still produce a different forecast change from the source. Conversely, two forecasts might move together because both respond to the same recent observations. Measure both comparisons and benchmark what a method given only the input or forecasts can do.

Two additional distinctions matter. A template for a cue can reproduce its average effect while missing the variation from one series to another. A high cosine can also conceal a negligible effect or a badly wrong dose. Keep the target metric in forecast units alongside agreement in signed horizon shape.

## Experiments

The boundary experiment measures what is lost at the SAE threshold. The transport experiment tests history-specific edits on time-aligned patches. The behavioral controls measure forecast agreement under input edits. The fixed-direction steering experiment asks whether a single reusable edit can move between models and whether its transfer changes with depth. Each experiment records its own data split, endpoints and controls before evaluation. The final report links the measurements and states what remains open.

## What the published experiments actually tested

This targeted review covers primary papers available by 20 November 2026. I searched for combinations of “time series foundation model”, individual checkpoint names, “sparse autoencoder”, “feature alignment”, “activation transplantation”, “model stitching” and “steering”, then checked methods in the original papers. I included single-model SAE work, comparisons on shared inputs, and studies that actually change the running forecaster. The closest SAE studies on pretrained TSFMs were three papers at the ICLR 2026 TSALM workshop.

Relevant comparisons already exist. Wiliński et al. compared MOMENT, Chronos and Moirai, found blocks of redundant representations, and showed that activation-derived concept directions could steer periodicity or trend. Bao et al. found that their tested TSFMs tolerated removal of entire layers and identified individual attention heads associated with copying motifs or seasonal bias. Aionoscope applied the same synthetic-state readout to 37 model/adapter systems. Component presence was often accessible, but the best dense-state row reached mean masked R² of .689 against .999 for the oracle. In Yang et al., the same TimesFM 2.5 trunk gave different trend gains from its median and mean heads (0.92 and 0.65). A model can encode a variable and make a distinct choice about how to use it. [Wiliński et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wilinski25a.html) · [Bao et al., revised paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01605) · [Aionoscope](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00956) · [Yang et al., table 26](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34316v1)

Other adjacent results inform the controls. Oublal et al.'s similarly named TimeSAE learns an explainer from inputs and outputs. TSAE reports lower feature-transition rates and forecast-reconstruction errors across three seeds on frozen PatchTST. SteerCast retrieves corrections expressed in a forecasting model’s own latent space. Language-model stitching has transferred SAE parameters, probes and steering vectors between checkpoints; it provides a methodological precedent in a different modality. [Black-box TimeSAE](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/oublal26a.html) · [TSAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04925) · [SteerCast](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11229) · [Language-model stitching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06609)

Two additional results sharpen the interpretation. Sanyal et al. transplanted statistics of calm and crash histories into Toto and Chronos and induced corresponding forecast shifts in both. The model computes its own donor statistics in each experiment. Jander et al. independently perturbed six synthetic generators for Chronos-2 and TimesFM 2.5 and found shared overpersistence and failures around regime switches. Similar behavior across models can therefore be measured without transporting a single feature direction. [time2time, methods and models](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05801v1) · [Jander et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24303)

Negative SAE findings exist as well. On a narrow, supervised PatchTST, Yıldırım varied the dictionary from half to four times the FFN width. The reported mean downstream performance change was 0.214% and dominant-feature interventions moved forecasts little. The model, activation site and training regime differ from the pretrained TSFM residual streams used here. In language models, Paulo and Belrose showed that changing only an SAE’s seed could change which features were learned, particularly with TopK. Subsequent work found that different individual features can occupy reproducible subspaces. Those findings motivate measuring both one-feature correspondence and what a collection of directions can express. [PatchTST experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05151v1) · [Same-data seed comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16615) · [Subspace comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12138)

A separate public technical report by Nabin Prasad Dev tested whether Chronos-T5 SAE codes help predict forecast difficulty beyond eight input statistics. On the main ETTh1 channel, the larger model-size control also failed to show an improvement. Removing five difficulty-associated features had initially raised hard-window CRPS by .043. Repeating all conditions with 100 forecast samples reversed this to −.049 [−.086, −.015], while the reconstruction-only control was consistent with zero. The changed sign weakens the original causal interpretation. This study compares signals for routing forecasts across model sizes and illustrates how the forecast sampling procedure can influence an apparent SAE effect. [Author’s technical report, §§4.12–4.13](https://github.com/nabindev3/tsfm-sae-difficulty/blob/main/eval/report.md)

A 21 November follow-up search found two relevant October preprints. Bourgeat compared removing blocks in Chronos-Bolt and TimesFM 2.5 on 1,088 held-out forecast windows. Moderate skipping preserved average forecast quality, while equal-budget masks favored different windows and did not generalize consistently across datasets. This tests which computations an ordinary forecast needs. Our layer experiment instead measures where a specified intervention can control a forecast. Bai found that linear probes could decode a generating regime and intervention effects from time-series embeddings even when the models’ predictions responded to confounded associations. That provides an independent reason to test what a forecaster does with a readable feature. Their interventions concern external covariates, while our causal claims concern changes inside a frozen model. [Bourgeat, structured block skipping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13208) · [Bai, covariate audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31869)

The closest methodological precedent I located is a language-model preprint that explicitly compares per-feature ablation signatures and actually substitutes a second SAE’s feature contribution in Pythia and GPT-2. It reports strong matches among the highest-ranked features, with correspondence quality declining as coverage expands. Comparing the top 100 and top 2,000 matches led to different relative results for greedy and transport-based matching. That cautions against generalizing from a few appealing pairs. Hoang et al. also varied context while removing the same language-model feature and found that reusable one-dimensional logit effects were rare. The TSFM experiment here studies continuous forecast horizons, original/alternative dictionaries on fixed checkpoints, and different forecasting architectures. Measuring unobserved leads makes extrapolation testable separately from fitting a causal signature. [Cross-seed substitution benchmark, primary manuscript](https://openreview.net/pdf/fcff57610dd90ebc91f648853742309e00735280.pdf) · [Hoang et al., downstream effects across contexts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24645)

**Gap addressed here.** Among the TSFM papers reviewed, I did not find an independent study that removed a naturally firing source SAE feature, mapped its write across different forecaster architectures, and compared *both* models’ signed, multi-lead forecast changes against feature-identity and level-only controls on new histories. The public TSFM-Lens results above belong to this repository. We use them as prior evidence from this project.

The follow-up discriminated three explanations for a failed cross-model intervention. Level can pass around an intermediate token stream; recent relative shape has a different pathway. A full target feature knockout can dramatically overshoot a correctly signed direction. Finally, the two models' responses to a fixed write vary across histories: a controller informed by the source's current forecast response reproduced detailed shape in held-out series, including through several firing target SAE features. The open gap is **mechanistic correspondence under unseen conditions**, especially prediction of unmeasured leads at the right dose and generalization to rarely firing features. [Hypotheses, interventions and remaining limits](shape_transfer/README.md)

The 21 November experiments test that gap against self-transfer, a third donor checkpoint, changes of forecast origin, unseen leads and native prediction intervals. An intervention can be identified inside a finite dictionary, reproduced on specified outputs, or generalized to an unmeasured condition. These outcomes give different answers in the same models.
