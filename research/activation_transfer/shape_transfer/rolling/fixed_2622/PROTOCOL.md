# Prospective confirmation of TimesFM donor 2362 → Bolt decoder 2622 on traffic

Freeze before selecting new sensors and examining their codes or forecast outcomes. The initial rolling traffic discovery found that a previously selected Bolt direction still produced the desired eight-lead response after its coefficient left TopK. Most cases involved TimesFM donor 2362 and Bolt direction 2622. This confirmation asks whether directly selecting that fixed direction, without conditioning on it ever winning the local competition or being active, recovers the new donor ablation. This is a single-donor, single-receiver-atom hypothesis chosen after discovery.

Sample 96 unseen Monash traffic sensors, excluding every identifiable prior traffic ID included in the original rolling study exclusions plus its 96 discovery sensors. Reject nonfinite or constant windows and any standardized-history duplicates shared with the prior cohorts or new sensors. Use two pairs of 480-observation windows per sensor. The first pair matches the discovery tail schedule, 32 observations apart, with an eight-observation trailing reserve. For the second pair, move both windows earlier by a pregenerated random integer uniformly distributed from 168 through 335 hours per sensor. Thus the second pair is a phase-sensitivity test on the same sensors. Within each pair fix the origin mean/SD for both windows. Retain unmodified observed inputs and explicit original time indices. Treat sensors, not windows, as the statistical unit. The two phases overlap in calendar history and are never pooled as independent samples.

Before any endpoint evaluation, fit the fixed direction's signed per-unit donor-code scalar on the 96 original daily calibration histories by least squares against the source and recipient weighted eight-lead derivatives among cases where that donor fires. This rule makes no test-time derivative query and never gates on the recipient code. Fit separate calibration scalars for the original 16 donor atoms for descriptive donor-specificity checks. Freeze a target strength budget for each candidate equal to the same historical 20% target-state radius plus an anchor natural-coefficient bound. If that candidate is not firing on the anchor, substitute its median positive coefficient in the old calibration histories. If it never fired on old calibration, use the calibration median across all positive coefficients. For target 2622, choose three other fixed target IDs without looking at validation states or outcomes: nearest in calibration response magnitude (full and centered RMS across all calibration derivatives) and unconditional activation frequency. Standardize each of the three matching descriptors by its calibration-dictionary SD before Euclidean distance. The matching controls have their own old calibration fit and anchor bound.

At the new origin physically execute (a) the calibration slope times the old donor strength; (b) calibration slope times the current donor strength; (c) the optimal anchor-only signed dose on fixed 2622 against the anchor donor waveform, carried unchanged even when 2622 is inactive; (d) that exact dose gated on current target membership only, without clipping its magnitude; (e) the anchor-only fixed dose rescaled by the current/old donor strength; (f) a new signed dose on the same fixed direction using current source and target derivatives regardless of support; (g) current best of all 32 naturally firing target atoms under the original bounds; (h) the original all-32 anchor winner and anchor dose carried unchanged; (i) anchor-only local fits for the three calibration-matched fixed directions carried unchanged. The off-support policies share the same per-candidate anchor budget and historical radius cap. The local all-32 comparator uses current natural bounds. The source reference is removing the donor at its natural **current** activation in the current forecast; never use it for fitting an ID or dose. Measure all on-support and off-support effects as actual median forecasts.

Primary population: every sensor where 2362 fires at anchor and current origin, regardless of the fixed target's support or whether it was the original winner. Separately report originally winning vs other target winner, original target on/off, and current target on/off. Report all where donor now fires as a sensitivity, setting anchor-based doses to zero if donor initially inactive. In both phases evaluate full and horizon-centered waveform recovery and paired whole-sensor bootstrap intervals with counts, compare anchor-fixed versus gate/fresh and the average of three matched alternatives. Show differences in actual amplitude and selection. Report every other frozen donor's firing count, per-donor estimates on at least eight persistent cases, leave-primary-out results and across-donor sensitivity only where at least two donor identities can contribute. The primary donor has no estimable donor-population uncertainty. Do not select another primary ID or dose after scoring. Report the earlier phase even if it reverses the conclusion.

Before any new checkpoint capture, add a second receiver-2622 scalar fit with the identical weighted least-squares formula on the 96 *discovery traffic anchor* histories, using only ordinary-input derivatives and the natural donor code at that earlier origin. The origin-32 outcomes that identified the hypothesis never enter the slope. Test the frozen in-domain scalar with old versus current donor strength in both new phases. This directly tests an inexpensive prior-domain policy without per-sensor derivative fitting. Separate it from the old daily-calibration scalar and the locally anchor-fitted dose.

After the first validation outcomes, check one specific control limitation. The matched decoder IDs and 2622 had identical zero calibration prevalence, but their anchor prevalence and allowed coefficient magnitude could differ on traffic. For each of the same three frozen target directions, use the sign that best decreases predicted anchor error and give it exactly the same physical hidden-vector norm as the actual anchor-2622 write on that sensor/donor. The sham may exceed its own natural anchor-strength bound; this is a physical, dose-matched off-support control. Re-execute on the same new origins and report both nulls. No new candidate is selected.
