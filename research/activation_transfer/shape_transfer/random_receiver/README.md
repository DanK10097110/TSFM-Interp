# Which part of eight-lead transfer requires a trained receiver?

A randomly initialized Chronos-Bolt could reproduce the *direction* of a TimesFM feature's forecast effect when the requested change was made about 100,000 times smaller. At the TimesFM feature's actual removal amplitude, both random receivers scored essentially zero. Pretraining made a large difference to the output change achievable with the same relative hidden-state budget. High agreement on a tiny requested change, by itself, gives little evidence that two checkpoints learned the same feature. [Registered design and numerical amendment](PROTOCOL.md)

We kept the trained TimesFM donor, selected SAE features, real histories, median forecast head and 20% write cap from the original study. Two config-matched random Bolt checkpoints and the trained Bolt each supplied their own ordinary eight-lead Jacobian and baseline forecast. The scored reference was TimesFM’s **measured** change when a naturally firing feature was removed. In the amplitude test a single scalar per receiver multiplied that reference on every held-out case. It was chosen on 96 other histories. The donor never received a physical removal at this smaller dose. The controller saw its pre-intervention derivative estimate only.

Daily results use 192 series and give each of 14 frequent donor atoms equal weight. A score of one is an exact match. Zero is the result of leaving the forecast untouched. The score removes each intervention’s horizon mean. For seed 7513, the independent replay placed daily small-task skill at .895 [.877, .923]. Its typical centered goal was about 532 times the measured numerical noise of a zero write. On 128 virtual-machine traces, the corresponding small-task scores were .795 and .777, with only two frequently firing donor atoms. Relative to its own clean state norm, the trained receiver had roughly 30,000 times the median output sensitivity of these random twins on daily data. A perturbation small enough for the random receivers to realize was therefore a materially different control task. The original 0.2%-radius finite-difference check failed on the first random initialization. Successively larger probes revealed float32 subtraction error at the small end of the range. Before looking at either receiver's steering score, we required all three adjacent 1%, 2% and 4% probes to satisfy the original 8% error threshold. The same checks were applied to the trained weights. These experiments describe two random initializations of one architecture, a particular readout and this bounded inverse. They do not establish a general lower bound for untrained networks. [Checkpoint and probe implementation](run.py) · [Independent solver](audit.py)
