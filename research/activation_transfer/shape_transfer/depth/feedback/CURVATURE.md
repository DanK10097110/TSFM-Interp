# Why did a handful of full-dose writes need shortening?

All ten eligible block-10 cases selected for a shorter scalar across the two VM cohorts involved Bolt feature 5876. This diagnostic is retrospective. Evaluate every original and additional VM on which 5876 fired, including those benefiting from no shortening. Compare its natural target write with the translations of the other 15 originally sampled donor decoder directions using the very same history and recipient. Scale every comparison to exactly the same target hidden norm as 5876, retaining the removal sign even for dormant source controls. Also use the opposite sign of 5876 and four seeded isotropic directions at the same target norm. None of the artificial alternatives is claimed to be a natural removal. Measure the absolute and relative error of the ordinary recipient's eight-lead Jacobian against the actual forecast at full and half of that target length. Compare the high-dose group (at least 5% of training radius) with smaller writes and report group counts. The larger raw write may itself select unusual histories.

For one influential original VM and one additional VM, identified by the preceding leave-one-machine-out analysis, trace the actual 5876 write as a fraction of its original size from 0 to 1. At each block downstream of the edit, capture the clean state and its differential along the chosen hidden write using centered steps of one and two percent of the original write. At full dose, substitute the extrapolated first-order trajectory for the *entire live block output*, then let the remaining blocks run. Verify that monitoring every block once leaves the ordinary output intact, that exchanging an actual full-dose block state reproduces the unmodified full write and that the two differences agree. These localized replays can implicate accumulation of nonlinear effects before or after a block. They do not establish which head or neuron implements them. This case selection can explain the earlier observation but cannot estimate prevalence.

## Results

In the original cohort, one of the 77 machines with an active 5876 write met the high-dose cutoff. In the additional cohort three of 170 did. At high doses, the norm of the 5876 forecast linearization error divided by the norm of its linear prediction was .604 original and .744 additional. Equal-length translations of the other donor directions also had substantial curvature (.540 and approximately .55). The same named comparison below the cutoff gave .037 and approximately .16. Weak-output random directions had far smaller absolute errors. This is consistent with a combination of write size, local sensitivity and history. Its identity alone does not explain the overshoot. At additional machine 697 those errors were .0847 untouched, .0584 after substituting block 18 and .0029 after substituting block 19. This motivated direct substitutions inside the final transformer.
