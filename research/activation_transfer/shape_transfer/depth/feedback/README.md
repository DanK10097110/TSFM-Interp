# A full-dose control failure traced to a small number of VM histories

The original .348 middle-layer result was real but a poor estimate of a general domain failure. One machine, `fastStorage_801`, dominated it. Leaving that machine out raised centered shape recovery to .927. With its source feature and natural dose intact, shortening the receiving write using feedback from TimesFM itself raised the full-cohort score to .930. On **256 previously unused machines**, middle-layer open-loop performance was already much better, .902. The same feedback procedure raised it to .970. The reference is Bolt’s actual full-dose removal of a firing SAE feature. The controller sees the ordinary-state Bolt derivative estimate and uses TimesFM's own forecasts to choose a write. The scalar search tries 0, ¼, ½, ¾ and the entire original target write, using the same target budget throughout. The last arm also computes the target derivative at the chosen state and tests one correction in a new direction. The chooser never sees the measured Bolt removal. The registered final-minus-middle contrast on the new cohort was +.069. Its history-only interval was [.001, .215]; resampling both histories and the eight qualifying source features gave [−.007, .350]. A universal preference for the last layer would overstate this result. The original and new cohorts had four and eight qualifying source features. Restricting the new cohort to the original four gave .834 initial and .969 scalar-adjusted middle-block skill, so changed feature eligibility explains only part of the cohort difference.

On the original cohort, only two of 127 qualifying writes selected a shorter length. On the new cohort, eight of 349 did. **All ten involved Bolt feature 5876.** Its own middle-layer skill rose from −1.352 on the original histories to .362 on the new ones before feedback. Its old outlier asked for 18.8% of the TimesFM training-state radius, within the 20% cap. The chooser halved the *target* step on that example. This fixes a specific overshoot while retaining the full-sized donor goal. It costs extra target forecasts, and matching the donor's predicted response more closely can hurt agreement with the actual donor on some cases. The high early scores also left the future open: on the new machines the corrected middle site recovered .064 of late shape; the corrected final site recovered −.009. Tracing the actual large writes identified a specific source of overshoot. At machine 801, extrapolating the clean tangent through block 18 still left .215 forecast RMS nonlinearity; extrapolating the block-19 output left .002. On the three additional high-dose machines, replacing the last feedforward SiLU alone by its clean local tangent reduced pooled RMS curvature from .081 to .037. Replacing the attention-branch normalizers gave .078 there, although they mattered much more at 801. Only four machines reached this dose and the component tests were selected after the original failure.
