# Can two receiving layers share the work?

Registered after seeing the Bolt single-layer sweep and before evaluating any two-layer outcome. Use the first and final output blocks, on the identical four discovery/replication cohorts and native donor feature removals. Both sites observe the same last 32 input steps. Each additive intervention acts on the live hidden state, allowing the first write to propagate through the final layer.

Normalize each site's coordinates by its own training-state radius, concatenate the two local Jacobians, and solve the same damped inverse on the first eight median leads. The reference is an actual source SAE removal. Compare a cap on the sum of the two relative write lengths with the original cap on a single site's relative length, at totals of .05 and .20. Also report a root-sum-of-squares cap at both budgets. The latter allows greater combined length if the work is shared. This is a common-ray cap after solving a quadratic objective. It does not optimize allocation under a sum constraint.

As an allocation control, give two slots to *the same* final layer. Their combined write has exactly the original single-site direction by the scale-invariance of relative damping. Under a sum cap, it reproduces the final-site result; under a quadratic cap the permitted combined length increases by a factor of √2. Test that identity on actual forecasts. Evaluate the first-eight centered skill, both single-site baselines, and the unconstrained next eight. Gains under the sum cap compared with both individual sites would suggest complementary control over these outputs, conditional on this choice of solver. Gains limited to the quadratic cap could come from additional total length. Also report realized site lengths and the relative size of the nonlinear difference between the actual combined change and the sum of its separately applied parts at .20.
