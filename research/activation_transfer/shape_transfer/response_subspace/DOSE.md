# Why does preserving the mean derivative leave forecast differences?

After inspecting the initial three Bolt dictionaries and one TimesFM dictionary, two explanations remained. An altered write can already act differently to first order on a new history even though its pooled calibration derivative is identical. Alternatively, a full feature removal can traverse a nonlinear portion of the network. Test both on the prespecified archive dictionary of each model, with the same four evaluation cohorts.

Compare the original native direction, its projections at ranks 8 and 32, and their original fixed norm-preserving rotated alternatives. Retain all original identities, signs and unchanging feature coefficients. Rerun each at exactly one quarter of its natural removal in the actual model. The reference is an independently executed quarter-dose native removal. Compare the full and quarter effects to each other after removing their scale; shrinking both writes never earns points solely for reducing amplitude. Reuse the ordinary-state eight-lead median derivatives for a zero-dose limit with known local sensitivity for each donor and every alternative. Report equal-feature, horizon-centered skill, the paired quarter-minus-full and local-minus-quarter differences, and native first-order error. Separately compare the pooled mean of the surrogate-minus-native Jacobian across all calibration histories to its mean conditional on the atom firing, its series-to-series RMS, and its value on testing. Pooled means can conceal errors with opposite signs. Treat these explanatory measures as diagnostics; no choices depend on the new evaluation scores.
