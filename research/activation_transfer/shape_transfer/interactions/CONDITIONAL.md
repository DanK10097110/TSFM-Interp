# Can we transfer the interaction once we measure it at partial doses?

The first Bolt → TimesFM factorial results matched joint effects well and missed their much smaller interaction. We registered a follow-up before any partial-dose outcomes or any joint measurements on the third group of 128 daily histories. Restrict to Bolt donors and TimesFM recipients, the direction with no clipping in the earlier daily singleton test. Use the original 192, second 128, and the separate 128 daily histories previously frozen for the blind-frequency study. The third group's joint and partial-dose responses are new. Use every coactive pair of the same 16 source features, retaining eight distinct series per reported pair.

The donor sees four partial combinations: either one quarter or three quarters of each removal. Four times the difference of differences on this interior square estimates the interaction of the full removals. It is exact for a bilinear dose interaction and can fail across changes of curvature. As a donor-only control also estimate it from the square between zero and a half dose of each removal. None of these probes measures the full dual source removal. Half-dose and interior-square estimates have the same fourfold normalization and square area. The full dual source removal stays reserved for scoring.

The receiver first implements each individually translated write plus their sum and measures its own factorial interaction, with the same common cap and zero patch. For the joint intervention only, add a hidden correction whose ordinary-state linear response matches the partial-dose donor estimate minus the measured receiver interaction. The two target singleton interventions stay identical, and the *total* corrected joint write is projected onto the original radius limit. Run the corrected target forecast on all 16 leads, matching only the first eight. Controls use a permuted donor lead order or request zero interaction. These methods spend three recipient factorial forwards in addition to the shared clean reference, and one extra receiver forward per correction, as well as four perturbed donor calls for the interior-square estimate. Corrections depend on the feature pair and its input.

Primary questions on the third cohort are whether the partial-dose donor estimator predicts full-dose centered interaction above zero and whether the corrected target improves upon both independently transferred writes and the zero-interaction controller. Report actual joint and interaction recovery, pooled absolute effect sizes, and pair-conditioned series intervals. The experiment tests whether additional information can control an interaction that single-feature steering left unidentified. It does not turn the original independent directions into a single transferable circuit.
