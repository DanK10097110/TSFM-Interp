# Is the translated edit too small to leave the target's current SAE support?

The endpoint study found zero support changes in 360 eligible Bolt → TimesFM daily writes, despite weak recovery of their centered forecast shapes when re-encoded. Here we test a specific geometric explanation. Normalize each of the same actual receiving directions to the checkpoint's allowable hidden radius, keeping sign and history. Find the first change of the receiving SAE's own clean active set along that line up to the published cap (20% of training radius). Compare the natural deployed length with the boundary distance and repeat for one fixed-seed isotropic direction per query. Compute the incidence of a change at 1%, 5%, 25% and 100% of that cap, and at the original deployed length. The endpoint's eight-firing feature rule remains in force. These are encoder-gate sensitivities; apart from the already tested deployed write, they do not imply a successful forecast intervention at a larger or arbitrary dose.

The daily Bolt → TimesFM write used a median .57% of the permissible length. None of 360 deployed writes changed the TimesFM SAE support. Following the same 360 directions further would eventually change it in 260 cases by the maximum radius. Among those, the median first change was at 57.0 times the deployed length. Isotropic directions at the same small lengths also caused no changes. TimesFM → Bolt used a median 54.3% of its receiving cap on daily histories. Of 455 deployed directions, 440 had already changed support; 439 of 455 norm-matched isotropic directions did too. At the shared maximum every tested direction in that model-domain cell crossed. The observed asymmetry reflects how large the deployed writes are relative to the particular receiving dictionary's local rank margins. The original weak centered result into TimesFM still occurred within a constant active set.
