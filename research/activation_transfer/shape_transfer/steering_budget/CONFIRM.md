# Fixed forecast-sensitive directions on new short daily and post-release weather histories

The original daily and traffic results motivated an external test. On 160 previously unused M4 daily histories of length 256, 19 unused histories of length 480 (descriptive), and the 46-station, four-origin NOAA USCRN 2026 panel, keep all receiving subspaces and calibration Jacobians frozen. Use the source features, desired response, history-specific actual displacement budget, normalized scale, public forecast and item filtering of the support-confirmation study. Use one shared regularized, norm-constrained solver for all bases. Compare native 32 active receiving directions, 32 training activation PCs and 32 fixed directions estimated from the 96 previous calibration histories' ordinary early forecast derivatives. Also test the first 8 and 16 directions of the same calibration basis to learn how much history-to-history sensitivity variation matters. Every arm still receives the current eight derivative rows at inference. No NOAA derivative enters the library.

Primary new contrast: forecast-sensitive rank 32 minus the active SAE span for early centered recovery of the dense receiving edit, on 256-observation M4 with Bolt and TimesFM as recipients. For weather compute the same contrast, normalized per input; resample entire stations, keeping all four dates together. Count distinct stations, rather than station-date rows, for the minimum of eight firings per feature. The donor's actual held-out second eight leads are secondary. Both sets of source features are fixed by earlier studies. Other contrasts measure calibration information or rank. Even perfect early control would not establish feature identity.
