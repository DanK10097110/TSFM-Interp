# Which 32 receiving directions reproduce a donor's forecast edit?

A fixed set of 32 receiving directions learned from forecast gradients on other histories reproduced most of what a full-dimensional receiving edit could do to the first eight forecast values. The effect survived a change of history length and a move from M4 daily series to 2026 US weather stations. The receiving model's 32 currently active sparse autoencoder (SAE) directions performed less well at the same maximum hidden displacement. Which method better reproduced the original donor's *next* eight values depended on the receiver and domain.

## The comparison

On each new history we erased one naturally active donor feature and saved the resulting 16-step forecast change for evaluation. A controller used the donor's ordinary local derivative for the first eight leads. The full-dimensional receiving solution turned that request into a bounded hidden vector. Each 32-dimensional alternative received the early change predicted for that vector by the receiving derivative, the very same per-event norm ceiling and the same shape/level weighting. Each alternative used the current receiving derivative and the same ridge solver with a Euclidean ball constraint. The response directions were fixed using 96 separate M4 calibration items; the activation PCs used 1,024 training items. No test series, donor ablation outcomes or late gradients entered either library. The SAE span has 32 directions per input, selected by its trained encoder. The response library uses receiver-side output information while the SAE and PCA libraries train on activations. This difference in the information they are given helps explain the ranking. [Initial design](PROTOCOL.md) · [Frozen confirmation design](CONFIRM.md) · [Library construction](../response_subspace/run.py)

**Recovery** is one minus the squared error of the predicted change divided by the squared size of the reference change. We remove each waveform's mean within the assessed segment, so an accurate constant offset alone scores zero. Each eligible donor feature receives equal weight. One is exact and zero is the unchanged forecast. Scores can be negative. “Dense” below means the actual unrestricted receiving edit; “donor” means a different checkpoint's actual feature removal.

The weather forecasts used degrees Celsius. Both the desired and the measured edit were divided by the same history's temperature SD. Uncertainty resamples complete series or stations and the eligible donor features; repeat dates at a station stay together. There are four seasonal dates and only three qualifying donor atoms into Bolt. Atmospheric spatial dependence and checkpoint/SAE sampling are outside these intervals. The 19 unused daily histories long enough for a 480-observation window also favored the fixed gradient directions; only four and two donor atoms respectively qualified. ## Why the choice of solver matters

On the earlier 128 daily histories with TimesFM as recipient, scaling down an unconstrained SAE solution to fit the budget gave .458 early dense recovery. Solving the actual constrained problem raised this to .572. Optimizing only centered shape reached .749, at the cost of control over the average forecast level. In Bolt the same three scores were .709, .679 and .812. The balanced constrained solution optimizes a weighted, regularized local error. An improvement on every nonlinear shape endpoint is not guaranteed. On these original daily histories the 32 forecast-sensitive directions reached .974 into TimesFM and .992 into Bolt. Their corresponding later *donor* scores were −.370 and −.153. The long-range behavior of a donor feature remains underdetermined by eight early output constraints. [All solver and component comparisons](results/summary.json) · [Independent constrained-optimizer test](test_budget_solver.py)

The fixed library learns where the forecast is sensitive. The current derivative says how to mix those directions for a particular history. A high early score establishes short-horizon controllability within the tested intervention site. Reproducing a dense receiving solution leaves open the harder question of reproducing the donor in full. On short M4 into Bolt, moving from .986 against the receiving solution to .638 against the donor exposes that distinction directly. The independent support experiment separates the choice of receiving axes from the choice of current derivative. [Support and derivative factorial](../support_confirmation/README.md)

## Reproduction checks

An independent calculation solved the reduced problem using the output-space normal equations and a scalar root finder, formed the active SAE span with QR, and added the result to live hidden states inside the public forecast call. Eighteen randomly selected events per receiver/cohort were checked across three receiving bases, two receivers and all three new cohorts (324 waveform comparisons). The largest discrepancy was below 0.000005 history SD. These checks use a fresh numerical solver and hook; they share the original archived current derivatives and history IDs.
