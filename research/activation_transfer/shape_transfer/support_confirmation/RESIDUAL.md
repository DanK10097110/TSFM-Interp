# How can the zero-ID and native Bolt spans recover similar forecasts?

**They share some useful geometric directions, and distinct orthogonal directions still recover considerable early behavior.** The 32 frequent nonnative features scored .690. Removing every projection of these candidate axes into the native active span and refitting at the identical hidden budget reduced skill to .599. The change was −.091 [−.111, −.072]. Removing a generic isotropic rank-32 span from the same nonnative axes yielded .684, a change of −.006 [−.013, .000]. These are exploratory physical interventions on the 160 previously scored shorter M4 histories. [Protocol](RESIDUAL_PROTOCOL.md) · [All estimates and intervals](results/residual.json)

Every candidate set has 32 rows. We orthonormalized each original span, subtracted its projection into the specified other span, and orthonormalized the remaining directions. For the residualized rows we retained singular values greater than 0.0000001 relative to the initial unit scale. Every residual span had rank 32 on every history. Projection can yield a direction outside the original candidate span. We then refitted its coefficients using the inherited relative-ridge solve and radial cap, with the same current eight-lead target Jacobian, fixed requested dense waveform and original per-case dense length budget. All outcomes below use actual 16-lead forecasts from the original Bolt receiver. [Physical interventions and vector archive](results/bolt_residual.npz) · [Implementation](residual.py)

The original zero-ID and native spans had mean squared principal cosine .222, compared with .061 for zero-ID and the fixed isotropic span. Across histories the median closest pair of principal directions made an angle of 15.45°, while the median middle angle was 68.32°. The native projection contained a median 27.55% of the squared norm of the *optimized zero-ID write*. For the zero-ID decoder rows, the median condition number was 3.18 before orthonormalization. It became exactly 1 after orthonormalization and 3.75 after removing the native projections, before the final orthonormalization. The median condition number of the restricted, shape-balanced eight-lead derivative improved from 145 to 105. The loss did not require any singular rank collapse. The optimized native versus zero-ID hidden writes had median cosine .320 across donor firings. Their actual centered early forecast effects had median cosine .998. Removing the native projection made the fitted hidden writes orthogonal to native to numerical precision; their forecast cosine remained .997. The main change was amplitude. Pooled centered early output RMS fell from .466 to .369 of the achieved dense response. The orthogonalized zero-ID span still beat the equally orthogonalized isotropic alternative by +.187 [.157, .218]. This is evidence for both useful shared geometry and multiple hidden directions producing similar *early* shapes under unequal output gain. [Hidden cosines, finite response amplitudes and paired scores](results/residual.json)

We also decomposed the **already fitted** zero-ID write into its projection inside the native subspace and its orthogonal remainder. With the original component amplitudes and no refitting, physically patching the projection alone scored .265, and patching the remainder alone scored .514. Refitting at the full budget in the orthogonalized span then reached .599. The two hidden vectors sum exactly. Their separately measured early output changes summed to within .00359 RMS of the original physical effect, compared to an original RMS of .14489. Skill depends on squared error and does not add across components. [Per-intervention physical replay](results/residual_replay.json)
