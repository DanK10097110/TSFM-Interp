# Where in the input is the remaining daily–traffic difference?

The common-coordinate assay found 43/128 daily versus 0/128 traffic firings for Bolt, and 28/128 versus 0/128 for TimesFM, when each series had recent relative mean +1.25 SD, final value +.5 SD and fitted last-32 rise zero. We will test where the remaining input structure influencing those named SAE outputs lies. This follow-up is exploratory on the same 256 histories. This protocol precedes any mixed-history model run or inspection of their hidden states.

1. Use only that high joint target. Pair the 128 daily and 128 traffic histories in their frozen manifest order. Repeat with the same daily order and the traffic order cyclically offset by 37, fixed here in advance. Work in individual history SD units. Subtract each original standardized history's least-squares projection on the original global mean, recent relative mean, endpoint and slope rows. The orthogonal residual splits into the early 448 points and the final 32. Its early subvector has zero mean. Its recent subvector has zero mean, zero endpoint and zero fitted rise.
2. Split the normalized residual into three independently replaceable quantities: unit-length early pattern, unit-length recent pattern, and fraction of total residual squared norm in the recent section. The two unit patterns retain their orientation and time order. For each paired daily and traffic series cross all three choices, giving eight inputs per pair. Add a common least-squares projection that gives the exact same high joint target. Set each block's norm from the chosen original energy fraction and the total energy left after the common projection. Preserve unit full-history SD and zero full-history mean before restoring the early-pattern donor's native input mean and SD. The two all-from-one-domain conditions must match the earlier common-high inputs and native SAE responses within numerical tolerance. Verify all five constraints on every hybrid. Report low-energy or otherwise infeasible original patterns explicitly.
3. Observe the actual SAE after an ordinary model forward on each hybrid. This intervenes at the input, with the original checkpoint and TopK dictionary throughout. Measure firing, preactivation and threshold margin, and report native strengths/cutoffs. Primary statistic is the paired average firing-rate difference between all-daily and all-traffic sources allocated to each of the three input components. Average each component's incremental change over all six possible substitution orders; the signed allocations must sum to the known firing-rate difference. Report all eight cell counts, paired series bootstrap intervals (2,000), and the pre-fixed re-pairing sensitivity. Also show replacing each component alone starting from the all-traffic input. Artificial hybrid waveforms quantify directional dependence of this specified intervention and decomposition. Domain labels combine many physical and dataset differences. No attribution of a real-world cause or of a unique network circuit.
